{\Large\bfseries Accepted Service Adaptation:\\ Tight Parametric Quotients and\\ Minimal Additional Writable Memory\par}
\vspace{.35in}{Anonymous manuscript for Operations Research\par}\vspace{.25in}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\textbf{Abstract.} Public renewal states can recombine while carrying different accepted continuation obligations. For positive additive payments and separable quadratic rewards, the unfolded problem is classical laminar allocation, and normalized memoization yields the same public-graph recursion. We characterize the residual representation problem. An occurrence-to-vertex quotient constructs the complete dual-price response with at most $3N$ global knots and $O(N^2+M)$ response storage; both orders are worst-case tight, and a coefficient-event implementation has polynomial rational bit complexity including exact sorting and comparison. For a fixed root promise, the reached price graph is a deterministic output transducer. Classical state minimization then gives the smallest equivalent reached machine, while contract structure implies ordered behavior classes, a minimum additional writable alphabet $K_*=\max_v c_v$, and a linear worst-case family. We separately report combined reached states and read-only response precision. A heterogeneous renewal model yields an exact memory-value frontier and a strict comparative static along every ordered mean-preserving radial spread of continuation caps. A graph-sized shared-table comparator and exact supporting cuts complete the service benchmark. Exact laminar, convex, and public-graph comparisons validate the representation and repeated-query use.
\par\vspace{.15in}\textbf{Keywords:} continuation participation; contractual memory; parametric resource allocation.
\par\vspace{.1in}\textbf{Subject classifications:} Programming: stochastic resource allocation; Dynamic programming/optimal control: contractual memory.
\par\vspace{.1in}\textbf{Area of review:} Stochastic Models.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
The accepted-service problem studied here sits inside classical laminar allocation, and normalized memoization of the occurrence-indexed reduction gives the same public-graph recursion. The paper's contribution is therefore not the existence of a scalar marginal price or memoization. It is a sharp representation theorem for the complete response on the compact graph. The quotient has only linearly many distinct knot locations, its quadratic total response-storage order is unavoidable in the worst case, and the coefficient-event compiler has polynomial exact rational complexity.

Execution is a different resource question. For a fixed root promise, the compiler generates a finite deterministic output transducer from a continuous control problem. Classical behavioral minimization yields its coarsest behavior-preserving partition. Contract structure then adds ordered price classes and a worst-case-tight linear bound on the additional writable alphabet. The public vertex, the writable symbol, and the read-only response representation are accounted for separately; $K_*$ is not presented as total controller complexity. Proposition~\ref{prop:three-layer-accounting} collects these resource layers and the post-compilation query cost in one audit statement.

For renewal agreements, the value of a limited alphabet admits an ordered allocation formula under heterogeneous probabilities and costs. Along every strictly ordered mean-preserving radial spread of continuation caps, insufficient deterministic memory has strictly increasing loss, while the exact alphabet requirement can jump at arbitrarily small spread. This is a radial comparative static, not a general convex-order claim.

These conclusions rely on a scalar additive commitment, separable strictly concave quadratic rewards, interval controls, exogenous acyclic public transitions, and public-state-sufficient caps. They do not assign the same finite-price guarantee to switching, coupled commitments, or continuous outer shared-table design. Those models and the previously established adaptation, sensitivity, and deployment results retain their assumptions and proofs in the preserved theory volumes. The computational evidence establishes exact agreement and representation behavior for the implemented methods; it does not establish universal algorithmic dominance or empirical service calibration.
