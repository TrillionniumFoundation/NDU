{\Large\bfseries Accepted Service Adaptation:\\ Compact Allocation Quotients\\ and Minimal Price Memory\par}
\vspace{.35in}{Anonymous manuscript for Operations Research\par}\vspace{.25in}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\textbf{Abstract.} A service provider can return to the same public operating state with different obligations inherited from past renewal reviews. We characterize when these obligations admit an exact finite-memory implementation. For positive additive payments and separable quadratic rewards without switching costs, the full-history problem is a classical laminar allocation problem. We prove an explicit occurrence-to-vertex quotient and show that normalized memoization yields a globally bounded family of payment responses on the public graph. A coefficient-event construction has polynomial rational bit complexity. A backward behavioral equivalence then characterizes the smallest writable alphabet implementing the unique optimum, including randomized exact-optimum controllers under an explicit information-accounting convention. For a heterogeneous renewal family, we derive an ordered memory-value frontier and prove that greater outside-option dispersion at a fixed mean payment strictly increases the loss from insufficient memory. A shared-table comparator has a graph-sized convex formulation, and fixed positive-release queries admit exact supporting cuts even at coincident bounds. Independent tree allocation, sparse convex optimization, and public-graph promise discretization provide algorithmic comparisons. Exact scale tests include more than two thousand public vertices and a separate breakpoint-dense family. The broader switching and certified-deployment theory is preserved in supplementary volumes.
\par\vspace{.15in}\textbf{Keywords:} continuation participation; contractual memory; resource allocation.
\par\vspace{.1in}\textbf{Subject classifications:} Programming: stochastic resource allocation; Dynamic programming/optimal control: contractual memory.
\par\vspace{.1in}\textbf{Area of review:} Stochastic Models.
\end{minipage}\vfill{Revision R30, September 23, 2026\par}

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
Classical laminar allocation and a compact public-graph implementation are two representations of the same accepted service problem. The quotient theorem identifies exactly when their normalized responses coincide; the coefficient-event argument supplies graph-input and rational bit bounds. The backward behavioral quotient answers a different operational question: which reached prices genuinely require distinct stored symbols. Cap heterogeneity can make the physical public state insufficient even when a much smaller price record suffices.

For renewal agreements, the value of a limited alphabet admits an ordered allocation formula under heterogeneous probabilities and costs. Increasing outside-option dispersion at a fixed mean payment raises that value, while the exact alphabet requirement can jump at arbitrarily small dispersion. This separates the engineering requirement for exact execution from the economic value of approximate retention.

These conclusions rely on a scalar additive commitment and separable rewards. They do not assign the same finite-price guarantee to switching, coupled commitments, or continuous outer shared-table design. Those models and the previously established adaptation, sensitivity, and deployment results retain their own assumptions and proofs in the supplementary theory volumes. The computational evidence establishes exact agreement on tested instances and comparisons for explicitly implemented methods; it does not establish universal algorithmic dominance or empirical service calibration.
