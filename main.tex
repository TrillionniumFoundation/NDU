{\Large\bfseries Neural Differential Utility:\\ Accepted Adaptive Service Control and\\ Certified Value-Gradient Learning\par}
\vspace{.3in}{Anonymous manuscript for Operations Research\par}\vspace{.25in}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\textbf{Abstract.}
Can a provider adapt an externally priced service protocol without reducing customer utility or service? We analyze persistent tiers, physical stock, costly reconfiguration, and participation at every review. Aggregate commitments identify stock under general demand distributions. A complete tree-edge criterion characterizes when a customer-neutral static protocol can be improved despite continuation participation; primitive curvature bounds quantify the gain, and absolute switching costs produce an exact friction threshold. A remaining-payment state explains why contractual memory can matter even without physical adjustment friction. Exact continuous-action benchmarks retain the accepted values of time, demand regimes, and inherited tiers relative to a reoptimized static protocol. For implementation, compatible scalar neural critics propose policies for graph-coupled, quartic, multistage problems, and rational residual certificates bound full-horizon loss. An equally informed value-only comparison, cached-preconditioned classical solvers, and shifted graph families expose both useful approximations and adverse transfer. A certified static gate prevents negative incremental deployment value. The analysis separates structural sources of accepted adaptivity from the statistical performance of the approximation method while making both independently auditable.
\par\vspace{.16in}\textbf{Keywords:} service-level agreements; inventory control; persistent adjustment; customer participation; continuation participation; value-gradient learning.
\par\vspace{.10in}\textbf{Subject classifications:} Inventory/production: service contracts; Dynamic programming/optimal control: stochastic control; Analysis of algorithms: approximation guarantees.
\par\vspace{.10in}\textbf{Area of review:} Stochastic Models.

\section{Introduction}
A service provider can revise both physical capacity and the service tier attached to it. A higher tier may earn a premium and expose the provider to greater indemnity; replacing an installed tier consumes resources. Adaptation is valuable only if its gains survive the customer's commitments and comparison with a properly optimized static alternative. The central question of this paper is therefore operational: when can an accepted adaptive protocol create value without lowering service or customer utility, which information and endogenous state create that value, and how can an approximate implementation be audited?

We study Neural Differential Utility (NDU) as joint control of physical activity and a persistent, externally priced contractual state. The tariff and accounting rule are fixed before optimization. This is an ordinary Markov decision process with an expanded state and action space, not a way to escape conventional stochastic control. The distinctive comparison holds the customer's utility, expected fill, and physical resource cost at the levels of an optimized continuous static contract. A common retainer, when present, changes participation levels but not the reported incremental provider rewards.

The institution is an observable, metered service protocol. A provider and customer agree on the tariff, the rule governing future revisions, and the required service before operations begin. This is a useful abstraction for auditable capacity or managed-service schedules, not a claim that all real service agreements make actions observable. We consequently also solve an extension in which the customer may leave after every review. There is no private type or hidden effort in either model; the extension relaxes continued customer commitment while leaving the tariff unchanged.

Our first contribution is an exact accepted information-value decomposition over continuous contract actions. A supporting-line argument identifies physical stock from the service and cost commitments themselves. The remaining acceptance constraint is a payment budget. Separate quadratic programs solve static, time-only, and time--regime rules; a verified Riccati recursion solves the full inherited-state problem. Every optimum is attained deterministically, including against public randomized protocols. In the canonical instance, the total gain is 0.63666527. Demand-regime information generates most of it; inherited-tier feedback adds 0.01929038 after regime information is already available. These are accepted values, not increments borrowed from an unconstrained provider calculation.

The second contribution gives primitive structure with an exit option at every review. On a scalar tree, marginal reward per payment unit must be nondecreasing from each parent to its child for the reoptimized static protocol to be globally optimal in the payment-neutral class. A violated edge supplies a two-review improvement satisfying every continuation promise. This is an explicit linear-time test on primitive marginal costs, rather than a restatement in terms of unknown optimal multipliers. Smooth adjustment gives a quantitative gain bound; absolute adjustment yields an exact two-review friction threshold. A remaining-payment state reconciles continuation participation with dynamic programming and separates contractual memory from an additional demand signal. The earlier exact customer-exit comparison and complete multi-tier inaction regions remain available in the companion.

The third contribution develops and tests a full-horizon implementation certificate. A graph-coupled quartic resource model joins genuinely multistage inherited-tier control to all continuation constraints. Scalar neural potentials yield compatible actors; value-only and derivative-weighted fits receive the same scalar oracle observations, each with an exact error bound. Training on small graphs is followed by changes in graph topology, weights, curvature, and horizon. Cached preconditioning, repeated timings, and rational certification are included in the comparison. Strong distribution shift produces adverse learned outcomes, which remain in the results. A certified static gate prevents a failed proposal from forcing a negative incremental deployment reward. The method certifies a submitted policy; it does not assume that derivative supervision or learning is always superior.

The paper follows one sequence: institution, accepted-control structure, exact economic comparisons, and certified implementation. The electronic companion contains current proofs, inaction structure, and diagnostic details. A separate, explicitly indexed historical supplement retains the full continuous-review, diffusion, constructive-approximation, and earlier computational material, without silently removing it from the review package. These extensions have different units or evidentiary scopes and do not validate the new jump-contract result by analogy.

\subsection{Related literature and the incremental contribution}
Inventory decisions and service contracts have long been studied jointly. \citet{Henig1997} embed an inventory model in supply-contract design, connecting contracted quantities, delivery, and replenishment decisions. \citet{LiangAtkins2013}, \citet{Sieke2012}, and \citet{Katok2008} examine inventory agreements, penalties, coordination, and review periods. Our contribution is not the observation that contract terms affect stock. We fix the externally priced menu, retain the installed tier as a costly state, and compare accepted contingent revisions with an optimized continuous static tier under the same customer commitments.

Dynamic responses under service-level agreements are also established. \citet{Hosseinifard2022} optimize dynamic stocking under ready-rate agreements. \citet{Caggiano2007} address multiechelon service-parts agreements, and \citet{Abbasi2022} analyze service levels in vendor-managed inventory. Here even the static-contract comparator can adjust stock with the regime. The incremental controls are contractual tier revisions and their information requirements, not access to a state-aware inventory policy denied to the benchmark.

\citet{NasserTurcic2019} analyze temporary contract adjustment when a retailer has a private demand forecast. Their information rents and revelation problem differ from our public-state, fixed-tariff protocol. \citet{PlambeckZenios2003} study incentive-efficient control with unobserved effort. We do not claim to solve those incentive constraints: our customer-exit extension imposes continuation participation, not private-information truth telling. Combined pricing and inventory optimization is likewise established \citep{FedergruenHeching1999}; demand here is exogenous rather than shifted by the chosen price.

Our monotone selections use \citet{Topkis1978}; hysteretic control has predecessors including \citet{HippHolzbaur1988}. Ordered hulls, occupation measures, Lagrangian bounds, and completion of squares are standard tools. The contract-specific contributions are stock identification under aggregate commitments, the accepted information decomposition, the complete continuation-compatible edge criterion, its primitive friction and curvature implications, and an acceptance-aware full-horizon audit. First-order optimality, normal-cone representations, Bregman decompositions, and strong-convexity bounds are standard convex tools \citep{BoydVandenberghe2004}, not independent novelty claims. Their assumptions and operational specialization are stated explicitly.

Derivative-weighted fitting follows Sobolev training \citep{Czarnecki2017}, and polynomial graph filtering follows \citet{Defferrard2016}. The retained quadratic graph critic calibrates an exactly analyzable subproblem. The new compatible softplus potential addresses a nonlinear multistage objective. Neither architecture is claimed as newly invented; the contribution is equally informed fitting, continuation-constrained deployment, and full-horizon verification with all adverse outcomes visible. Forward--backward value-process methods \citep{MaProtterYong1994,HanJentzenE2018} and Markov-chain approximation \citep{KushnerDupuis2001} support the retained continuous-time material, which is labeled separately from the new continuous-action acceptance certificate.

\section{Conclusions and operational implications}
The relevant benchmark is an optimized accepted static protocol, not an unoptimized tier or a provider relaxation. Under continuation participation, primitive marginal reward ratios reveal which intertemporal reallocations remain acceptable. Smooth and nonsmooth frictions have different implications for strict gain, and a promise budget can remain relevant after physical switching friction vanishes. These structural conclusions identify where adaptivity can matter before a learning method is selected.

Implementation requires a separate standard of evidence. The nonlinear tree certificates apply to whole contingent plans and are independent of how those plans were proposed. Learned critics can provide useful candidates, but their strong-shift failures and inconclusive seed intervals preclude a universal superiority claim. A static gate and a declared certified-loss tolerance turn those limitations into explicit deployment decisions rather than hidden experimental exclusions. A calibrated service-system application, endogenous tariffs, and private information are important further research directions; none is claimed to have been established by the present synthetic, observable-state experiments.
