\section{Computational Study}\label{sec:r49-study}
The pre-execution protocol specifies 64 exact-rational runs and 36 direct mixed-integer envelope solves. All inputs are synthetic, on the normalized domain $[0,1]$, with common reward $f(x)=2x-x^2/2$. We evaluate the algorithm in Section~\ref{sec:r49-boxes}, not a conditional target portfolio. Every reported interval uses an original-feasible contract and a complete checked upper cover. The computational record gives the generators, all raw cases, actual toolchain, certificate hashes, and independent checking times. Historical studies are preserved without reclassifying their conditional or heuristic outputs as global guarantees.

\paragraph{Paired comparison without changing the contracts.}
We use all 24 preceding harmonic-coarsening instances, including distinct caps and costs, zero curvature, positive opening charges, and up to 256 histories. The budget, catalog, root promise, and individual constraints are unchanged. Each new run has tolerance $10^{-3}$, at most 31 nodes, and four root-price refinements. These are the same node and price budgets as the earlier paired study; timings are not claimed to be matched across execution environments.

All 24 new intervals meet the requested tolerance, compared with 14 of the 24 retained harmonic intervals. Nineteen new intervals have zero rational width. The largest remaining width is $0.00013796$, below $10^{-3}$. Eighteen cases have strictly smaller upper bounds and strictly smaller gaps than the harmonic comparator; five are equal and one has a slightly wider interval, still below the requested tolerance. A stronger exact model does not require every interrupted search bound to dominate a differently partitioned relaxation. The reversal is retained as case 19. Table~\ref{tab:r49-paired} reports every case. The 12 small-case exact optima remain inside the new intervals. These results test the stronger model directly: they do not subtract an estimated dispersion error from a reduced solver's gap.

\paragraph{Many distinct histories with common eligibility.}
The next 16 instances have 16, 64, 256, or 1,024 histories, catalog sizes nine or seventeen, budget three, two declared seeds, nonuniform weights, continuously varying rational caps, positive opening charges, and curvatures including zero. The root obligation is nine-tenths of aggregate capacity. All have a single eligible catalog prefix. The exact algorithm closes every interval in one node with tolerance zero. Table~\ref{tab:r49-scaling} reports optimization and independent verification times separately. The polynomial statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:r49-frontier}; these finite measurements illustrate its behavior and do not establish a machine-independent runtime law.

\paragraph{Eligibility complexity remains consequential.}
Six additional 12-history instances have five to eight eligibility classes. Each is run at tolerances $10^{-2}$ and $10^{-3}$ and node limits 31 and 127, giving 24 specified runs. All twelve requests at $10^{-2}$ finish; at $10^{-3}$, two of twelve finish. Thus ten tighter requests remain unresolved, and every one is retained. At 127 nodes the unresolved $10^{-3}$ gaps range from about $0.0012862$ to $0.0059058$. Table~\ref{tab:r49-stress} gives both node budgets and exact exhaustive references; the full record also includes the easier tolerance. Increasing the node allowance weakly tightens these observed bounds, but the fixed budget does not remove the theoretical exponential dependence on eligibility complexity.

\paragraph{Independent formulations and semantic checks.}
The direct uneliminated formulation uses activation and support binaries, service tiers, branch lotteries, the exact promise, and supportwise realization ceilings. The computational record includes every solver time, node count, relative gap, envelope error, and bracket width. Exact reference values lie inside all available numerical brackets, allowing the stated numerical tolerance.

All 64 original-space certificates pass a separate checker. Exact regressions compare 564 grouped price bounds with complete book enumeration, verify 72 recovered fixed-mean allocations, ten common-eligibility global optima, eleven boundary global cases, and five multigroup intervals. Sixteen deliberately corrupted certificates are rejected, including errors in service prices, costs, corrections, anchors, budgets, and the target cover. These checks complement the proofs: agreement of two numerical implementations is not used in place of a coverage or weak-duality argument.
