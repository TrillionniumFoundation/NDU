\section{Conclusion}
A renewal contract can make the distortion of a service code endogenous to participation and the allocation of continuation payments. The continuous saturated solution combines a classical unbiased quantizer inside its alphabet with a costly contractual tail above it. That tail permits an off-cap highest level while preserving the Monge order needed for classical linear-work matrix search. The expected-versus-pathwise distinction is a difference in feasible contracts, not a newly discovered randomization primitive.

The nonsaturated results give both a boundary map and a positive robustness conclusion: exact symbol requirements vary with the promise, low promises need no branch-specific terminal memory, and a strict institutional advantage persists on an explicit interval below saturation. For finite certification catalogs, risk constraints and actual selected-level charges enter an exact joint design recurrence. These results distinguish continuous uncharged optimization, promise sensitivity, and charged catalog optimization without conflating their guarantees.
