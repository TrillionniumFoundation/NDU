\subsection{Linear-work search on the same complete frontier}\label{sec:r36-evidence}
Table~\ref{tab:r36-scale} compares the implemented linear search with the unchanged monotone search on identical inputs, through 2,048 branches and eight budgets. Both expected and pathwise objectives agree exactly at every size. The new suite adds 100 rational instances, 4,232 old-algorithm objective equalities, 2,116 controller replays, and 43,431 exhaustive submatrix row-minimum checks. A direct branchwise comparator checks 156 reduced-candidate frontier values without production moments or dynamic programming. The companion records all test categories and their limits.

The asymptotic improvement does not imply uniform speed. At 16 branches the new expected-participation search makes 534 economic queries versus 289; at 2,048 it makes 128,966 versus 135,597. At the latter size its deterministic count remains larger: 163,838 versus 153,180. Repeated queries are counted, not hidden behind a cache. Actual runtimes and all exact codebooks are retained; no unexecuted quadratic case is reported as a timeout. This study isolates complete-frontier matrix search, not priority over unimplemented specialized optimization software.
