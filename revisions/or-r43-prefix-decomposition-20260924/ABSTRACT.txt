A renewal provider must allocate accepted obligations through a limited execution alphabet. We study joint alphabet and promise design with local participation limits, bounded realization exposure, heterogeneous shortfall costs, and charges for enabled levels. An eligibility-prefix reduction gives the exact response to any branch promise. Our main algorithmic result converts the price relaxation into an ordered path problem, solved in polynomial time with variable branch count and alphabet budget. The same decomposition supplies completion bounds for finite global search and independently checkable intervals when search stops early. For rational quadratic primitives, we retain an exact unrestricted continuous reference algorithm. Feasibility-preserving rounding connects catalog bounds to continuous values, and a fixed-input lower bound proves the uniform mesh rate sharp. The sharper saturated theory retains cap anchors, an off-cap highest level, and linear-time dynamic-programming layers after contractual tail minimization. A monotone-charge theorem separates a genuine institutional boundary from a specially shaped randomization example. Synthetic computations compare exact rational methods, report enumeration and arithmetic growth, and cross-check a separate original-policy nonlinear formulation. The decomposition extends to an abstract ordered-interface allocation class without requiring the renewal interpretation.
