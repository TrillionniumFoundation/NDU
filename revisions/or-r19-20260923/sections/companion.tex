\section{Current study: primitives, audits, and reproducibility}\label{ec:r19-details}
\subsection{Primitive construction and the scope of the experiment}
The 63-node binary tree has two service coordinates at every node. An unconditional coefficient for service $a$ at node $j$ is $c_{ja}=t_{ja}2^{5-\operatorname{depth}(j)}$, with integer $t_{ja}\in\{8,\ldots,12\}$. Outside tiers have denominator 32 and numerators from 13 through 19. Diagonal curvatures have numerators 8 through 24 and denominator 16; marginal reward offsets have numerators $-8$ through 8 and denominator 32. The six-by-126 resource map has integer entries $-4$ through 4 divided by 32. Four nonnegative cross-service capacity rows have integer coefficients 0 through 4 and right-hand sides with denominator 8. All initial installed tiers are one half. These values are generated once using primitive seed 15001; the resulting exact arrays, not a verbal generator alone, are supplied.

Every nonroot subtree has the outside protocol's payment cap separately for each service, while each service's root payment is fixed. The displacement vector therefore obeys homogeneous continuation inequalities and two root equalities. The outside protocol is heterogeneous and is not asserted to solve a static optimization problem for this primitive. Context reward numerators are independent integers from $-16$ through 16 divided by 16, and the positive friction numerator is from 2 through 32 divided by 64. The coefficient of each absolute switching difference has a numerator from 4 through 8 and denominator 8. Primitive heterogeneity does not itself constitute field calibration.

The derivative in Theorem~\ref{thm:r19-global} uses the six reward coordinates, not the scaled friction coordinate. Training rescales friction to a centered unit interval; all derivative targets are resource vectors in the reward coordinates' original units. The 192 tanh random features include a constant and the seven linear input coordinates. The ridge parameter is $10^{-7}$. Quadratic models use the corresponding linear and quadratic monomials; radial-basis direct-price interpolation uses a cubic kernel and smoothing $10^{-7}$. All fitted coefficients and feature weights are serialized. The full generator, solver adapter, independent audit, and model definitions are retained in the R16 source directory.

\subsection{Feasibility and finite precision}
For each service and nonroot node, negate its weighted subtree displacement and divide by its positive payment coefficient to obtain a transfer coordinate. Negative numerical transfers are truncated to zero and the rest are rounded downward on a grid with denominator $10^8$. The sparse two-entry-per-column transfer map reconstructs a rational displacement satisfying every root equality and continuation cap exactly. A common nonnegative radial contraction toward zero enforces all box and extra capacity rows. This construction works for the study's positive payment coefficients and homogeneous outside-relative commitments; it is not claimed for arbitrary signed tariffs. The general theorem permits any independently certified feasible response instead.

Tensions are clipped to their exact friction capacities; inequality prices are clipped to nonnegativity. Prices and multipliers use denominator $10^9$. The independent verifier recomputes box feasibility, subtree sums, root equalities, resource capacities, tension capacities, exact primal gain, and an outward-rounded conjugate upper bound. It uses only Python integers and rational arithmetic, with no numerical solver or matrix inverse. In particular it does not trust the proposal's active set, the repair routine's assertions, or the solver's termination flag. The gate compares exact gain with the known outside protocol only.

\subsection{Complete timing and offline accounting}
Each target tolerance has its own previous-context classical workspace. This prevents a second target tolerance from obtaining an uncharged within-context warm start. Cold solvers reset their iterate; all solver matrices and factorizations that the solver caches are already constructed before repeated decisions. Initial numerical tolerance is $10^{-5}$ for both full classical and price-response proposals. Actual full-objective refinement uses $10^{-9}$, initialized from the rejected proposal, with a new rational audit afterward. Prediction, response, repair, both audits when needed, and refinement are charged. The numerical tolerance is not the deployment certificate tolerance.

The first label in each cohort is cold; the subsequent offline labels use previous-solution warm starts. All 256 labels and all seven model fits are measured on the same host as the follow-up online run. Offline coefficients are not substituted for the already frozen deployment models. For a method with mean complete cost $t_m$ and the lower observed classical mean $t_c$, the descriptive break-even count is the ceiling of charged offline cost divided by $t_c-t_m$, only when $t_m<t_c$. The comparison with the lower-mean classical method is descriptive: associated Student intervals are not multiplicity-adjusted claims after comparator selection.

\subsection{Hard-regime stratification and retained adverse evidence}
The star reassessment has four training seeds, four explicit strata, 128 contexts per stratum and seed, and eight methods, yielding 16,384 method-context records. Methods share contexts within a seed. These are not 16,384 independent training replications. Stratum-level and geometry-level summaries are generated from every record; no stratum, zero-gain context, or failed immediate certification is discarded. The full machine-readable stratification also conditions on absolute distance to critical friction, active and saturated leaf counts, nearest breakpoint distance, and the certified lower optimum gain. Boundaries for these descriptive bins are declared in the analysis script; they are not confidence-based subpopulation discoveries.

\subsection{Verification scopes}
The published research policies undergo 16,384 independent rational replays. The new multistage geometry suite contributes 128 further policy/certificate replays on 64 instances. Its numerical matrix residual bound is checked against certified reference brackets, not called an exact-arithmetic proof of the matrix inverse. The separate 64-case quartic suite verifies both the exact decomposition and the inexact-response inequality using rational arithmetic throughout. The written theorems, rather than these finitely many instances, establish the general mathematical claims. All follow-up timing pipelines must satisfy their requested full-objective certificate. Build checks separately verify manuscript inputs, cross-references, page layout, archival preservation, and the exact published revision SHA.
