\section{Computational Evidence and Decision Cost}\label{sec:study59}
The study separates three questions: whether the reductions and recovery identities agree with independent exact calculations; how a matched end-to-end allowance is consumed by solving and checking; and which numerical parameters obstruct the theoretical algorithms. It does not test adoption by a provider. The operational architecture and every earlier experiment remain in the retained complete R58 readers.

\subsection{Exact structural checks}
The parameterized reduction is tested on 4,240 candidate books, including books that omit baseline commands or install multiple options from a group. Fifty-one values are cross-checked against the existing rational fixed-book allocator. Forty-eight small graph instances compare clique existence with the full no-carry group encoding. The cap-preserving prototype algorithm agrees with exhaustive medoid selection on 160 group-budget frontiers. These finite tests supplement the general proofs; they are neither a proof by experiment nor independent evidence of real-world cap or reward clustering.

\subsection{Matched resources and stronger formulation}
The new frozen panel contains eight seeds for each of eight one-factor cells. The baseline has 24 histories, 17 catalog points and a budget of four. Other cells vary the catalog to 33 and 65, histories to 96, budget to two and eight, and additive tolerance from one hundredth to one twentieth and one five-hundredth. Probabilities are nonuniform normalized positive integers; promises cycle through one quarter, one half and three quarters of aggregate capacity. All inputs, distinct cap counts, joint reward-type counts, probability-denominator bit lengths and predicted grid sizes are recorded per instance. Eight additional group-sum instances use an exact target and two to five groups. A structured exhaustive oracle supplies their exact optimum, without treating equal numbers of lexicographic books as equal work.

Each of the 72 instances is assigned price search, the deficit algorithm, enumeration, and the direct mixed-integer convex formulation in Section~\ref{sec:form59}. All 288 requests receive three seconds from process creation through independent checking, with an internal optimization cutoff at 1.8 seconds. Execution is sequential and method order is deterministically shuffled within each instance. The deficit implementation retains the declared 600,000-state guard; rational lattice sizes are reported both numerically and in bits. A state guard or deadline is an outcome, not a removed observation. This short-budget panel measures an explicit resource regime, not asymptotic runtime or the best performance achievable with longer solver tuning.

The SCIP formulation retains the exact quadratic service epigraph rather than a piecewise-linear service envelope. Its formulation error is zero; its numerical feasibility tolerance and global bounds are not rational proofs. A selected book is reconstructed by exact allocation, and that original-space policy is checked independently. The common accuracy target is an additive difference in the same net-value units. Rational and numerical target attainment occupy separate columns; the latter uses a stated reporting tolerance of $10^{-8}$. An optimizer returning a narrow interval after the total deadline does not count as a completed decision. Independent checking, serialization, startup and model construction all consume the common allowance.

\subsection{Screening and limits of interpretation}
The earlier matched screening study remains a negative economic result: preprocessing improved total optimization, serialization and checking time in none of 18 pairs, with a median ratio of about 1.604. A safe removal rule is therefore not automatically an economical preprocessing rule. We retain its mathematical guarantee and all raw observations, but do not cite removal counts as evidence of end-to-end speedup. Screening is not activated in the new primary panel. A deployment decision would need to compare its fixed overhead with downstream savings on the actual workload.

The new panel varies more dimensions and adds an exact convex formulation, but remains a deterministic synthetic engineering study. It is not independently generated field evidence, a statistically representative sample of applications, or proof that branching depth is controlled in general. The parameterized theorem is the explanation for why a bounded command budget does not itself yield a fixed-parameter algorithm; experiments cannot replace that distinction. Complete row-level outputs, checker high-water memory and resource-limit records support inspection beyond aggregate medians.
