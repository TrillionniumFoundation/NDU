\section{Conclusion}
Endogenous command support can be separated from continuous allocation without discarding individual feasibility. The selected-boundary construction gives implementable path payoffs; capacity conservation identifies when sources can share a deficit coordinate; and repair on the same path converts a numerical grid into an exact-promise policy. The original model is nevertheless parameterized-hard jointly in command budget and cap count. Small-type compression therefore gives a meaningful support theorem and an XP algorithm, not a general fixed-parameter solution.

This frontier leaves useful design choices rather than a single universal solver. A common-curvature prototype frontier trades a certified payoff loss against a smaller common catalog. Conservative paths admit additive guarantees even when exact optimization is obstructed by binary encoding. A direct convex formulation and rational path certificates supply different forms of evidence and incur different costs. The preserved unfavorable screening result illustrates why safe preprocessing and economical decision support must be evaluated separately. The mathematical scope remains the original accepted-promise problem; extending feasibility inputs to uncertain or endogenous distributions requires an additional model, not an unproved transfer of the present guarantees.
