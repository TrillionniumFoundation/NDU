A renewal provider must choose a limited command alphabet and allocate an accepted obligation across heterogeneous histories. We develop finite-catalog joint design with opening charges, expectation caps, and realization ceilings. Terminal-first pooling retains individual caps and service costs while assigning an exact correction to the last eligible selected command. Common catalog eligibility therefore permits exact polynomial optimization with a variable symbol budget. Because unused catalog insertions can split eligibility classes, we also develop a mandatory-selected-prefix price recurrence and a certified completion search. This search turns the inherited fixed-budget enumeration regime into a prunable original-budget algorithm without an outer search over all eligibility-class means. We characterize catalog refinement, exact screening, signature-cell stability, and robust design under uncertain ceilings. Explicit dyadic counts support the complementary group-mean accuracy scheme. Independent checks certify original-space policies and complete search covers. Synthetic experiments include strict interior prefixes with 1,024 histories, matched class counts, refinement paths, and direct mixed-integer diagnostics. All six previously difficult instances receive zero-width rational certificates; all interrupted requests are retained. A stochastic capacity-reservation model derives the same correction from expected served demand.
