\subsection{Training samples, derivative labels, and uncertainty}
The graph critic uses degree twelve and thirteen fitted readout coefficients. Training seeds are 101 through 110. Each seed generates 96 graphs of size 32 or 64 and three scalar observations at each graph's central and perturbed inherited-state vectors. Value-only training uses all 288 scalar observations. Value--gradient training uses the identical observations and adds their centered directional-difference residual to the fitting objective. It does not receive neighboring exact values unavailable to the comparator.

For the quadratic oracle and quadratic critic features, centered directional differences equal the exact directional derivative for every nonzero step. Consequently the derivative-weighted objective is also a finite-difference regularizer constructed from the same scalar information. Calling these two descriptions independent baselines would double count one method. The sensitivity file evaluates steps 0.01, 0.05, and 0.1 for the predesignated seed 101 on all four 1,024-service test graphs. The main step is 0.025. There is no teacher spatial grid or discretization-dependent differentiation rule in this experiment; the historical continuous-chain experiment retains that separate limitation.

Test graphs use the fixed seed 9091, four graphs per size, and coupling strengths one through four. Their topology is a cycle with an additional random matching. Tariff numerators are sampled from the integers 36 through 316 and divided by 100. The matrix is supplied in integer CSR form; the test arrays, deployed tier numerators, dual numerators, and fitted coefficients are retained. Training graphs and test graphs are distinct. The model is not retrained for 256 or 1,024 coordinates.

\subsection{Arithmetic and timing scope}
Timing uses one CPU environment with BLAS and OpenMP thread counts set to one. Each recorded time is the median of three calls containing the two required linear solves or graph-filter applications and customer-budget calibration. Offline sample generation and fitting, graph construction, and the rational post-deployment audit are excluded from that deployment time. Average value--gradient sample generation plus training is approximately 0.087 seconds in this environment. Hardware timings are observations, not complexity guarantees.

The learned readout stores thirteen float64 coefficients, or 104 bytes, in addition to the graph and the working vectors needed by every method. A dense matrix alone at 1,024 coordinates stores 8 MiB in float64; this is an accounting comparison, not a claim that a sparse direct solver uses a dense matrix. The graph and its factorization are not free. Sparse direct, dense Cholesky, conjugate gradients with relative tolerance $10^{-4}$, and a fixed analytic degree-twelve Chebyshev inverse use the same deployment graph, tariffs, and acceptance calibration.

Each tier and multiplier is rounded to denominator $10^{10}$. A downward correction ensures the payment inequality using exact fractions, rather than a floating tolerance. The independent checker confirms matrix symmetry, nonpositive off-diagonal entries, and a row sum of 21 for the integer matrix. Thus the matrix divided by ten is bounded below by $2.1I$. It recomputes the residual norm and dual slack with arbitrary-precision integer arithmetic. Stored reward and bound fractions must match exactly. The certification concerns the deployed rounded policy and the continuous-action quadratic problem; it neither treats floating learned weights as exact real numbers nor certifies a general continuum HJB.

\section{Result-specific novelty and evidence map}
The stock-support lemma and accepted quadratic reduction establish an economic identification result. Convexity and Riccati algebra are standard; the new assertion is that the same physical commitments force a policy reduction and permit an exact accepted information decomposition. The local perturbation theorem supplies primitive conditions for a customer-neutral improvement. The continuation program and its feasible rational policy quantify the cost of allowing customer exit.

The lattice theorem uses standard monotone-selection machinery under contract-specific compatibility. The ordered envelope exploits a known one-dimensional upper-hull construction; the quadratic grid theorem has its stated contract-discretization scope. Multi-tier inaction intervals use every competing action. The Bregman formula is a dual-coordinate identity, not a fast multidimensional hull theorem. The occupation formulation and Lagrangian upper bound use standard constrained-control duality; their role is independent certification of the economic comparison.

The learned graph critic combines an established polynomial representation with compatible value derivatives and acceptance calibration. Its architecture and least-squares readout are not claimed as new general-purpose learning algorithms. The new evaluation is equal in raw information, transfers across service dimensions, and supplies exact post-deployment loss bounds.
