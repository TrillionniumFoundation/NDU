## 1. The accepted information decomposition is now solved, not inferred

**Comments 2, 14, 18.1, and 19.2.** The earlier inherited-state increment came from the provider relaxation. It did not identify how much inherited information was worth under customer acceptance.

The new section on accepted adaptivity solves four continuous-action problems under exactly the same customer utility, expected fill, and physical-cost reservations. Their values are:

| Contract information | Accepted provider reward | Increment |
|---|---:|---:|
| Static | -5.870110973046760 | — |
| Time only | -5.869294096264034 | 0.000816876782726 |
| Time and current regime | -5.252736080200586 | 0.616558016063448 |
| Time, current regime, inherited tier | -5.233445701435326 | 0.019290378765260 |

These are rounded displays of rational optima, not LP solver tolerances. The proof first identifies stock using a supporting-line inequality: the static fill lower bound and physical-cost upper bound force every feasible stock choice to equal the canonical stock. Only then is stock eliminated. The remaining customer constraint is a net-payment budget.

Static, time, and time–regime controls are separate strictly concave quadratic programs with shared variables for their permitted information. The full problem is solved by a Riccati recursion whose affine actions are verified at both endpoints of the entire inherited-tier interval for every period and regime. Nonnegative rational multipliers, stationarity, feasibility, complementary slackness, and forward moment calculations give matching upper and lower values.

The inherited-tier contribution is about three percent of the total accepted gain. Most value comes from current-regime reallocation. We state this directly, and do not attribute the full gain to memory. The older unconstrained increment of 0.050593409253 remains visible as a different calculation.

## 2. Randomization is treated at the protocol level, and the continuous optimum is deterministic

**Comments 13, 15, 18.1, and 19.4.** The original exact certificate remains explicitly a 0.1-tier-menu result with the stated stock menu and a continuous static comparator. We neither relabel it a continuum certificate nor suppress its one-state lottery.

The new hierarchy permits continuous tiers and continuous stock. The stock-support proof extends from the complete set of demand knots to the stock interval by piecewise linearity. A public random tape, independent of exogenous demand, defines mixtures of the restricted protocols. Conditional averaging over that tape preserves their information restrictions and linear customer payments while lowering convex maintenance and adjustment costs. This proves derandomization for static, time-only, time–regime, and inherited-only classes. The full class is handled by the verified deterministic Markov optimizer and a matching bound over all randomized history-dependent policies; a naive conditional-mean Markov argument is not used.

The continuous deterministic optimum gains **0.6366652716114345** over the optimized static contract, exceeding the coarse randomized optimum by **0.01152495963928**. Thus neither the coarse grid nor its lottery generates the economic effect.

We also solve menus with 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, and 320 intervals, report numerical randomized primal–dual values, and retain deterministic feasible incumbents. At 320 intervals the deterministic incumbent gains 0.6366332007624527. We do not call those extracted deterministic incumbents globally optimal; the less favorable 20-interval extraction is visible. The continuous deterministic theorem supplies the stronger conclusion without claiming a difficult discrete deterministic MIP was solved when it was not.

**Location:** accepted derandomization proposition; exact-hierarchy theorem; main fine-menu table; `robustness.py` and `results/fine_menus.json`.

## 3. Robustness is studied on the accepted problem, including adverse cases

**Comments 3, 13, and 19.3.** The R7 design has 31 accepted full-stock programs. It varies adjustment cost, maintenance coefficient and power, indemnity, premium intercept and slope, service valuation, regime persistence, demand dispersion and probabilities, utility and fill reservations, and stock resolution. Tier resolution is varied separately.

Each primitive perturbation receives a newly optimized continuous static reference. Where a reservation is tightened, that original reference can become infeasible. The output records this fact, and the accepted static tier is reoptimized where the payment-budget reduction applies. A positive difference from an infeasible comparator is not called accepted dominance.

Twenty-nine programs are feasible; twenty-eight have positive gains over the applicable accepted continuous static comparator. The zero regime-premium-slope case has zero gain. A fill-floor increase of 0.015 is infeasible under the original cost cap and remains infeasible under the tested cap increase of 0.6. These cases are retained. Floating-point flow, inequality, reduced-cost, and primal–dual residuals are recorded; these are not claimed to be exact rational certificates for all perturbations.

We add a primitive theorem, rather than asking a parameter sweep to prove robustness. At an interior constant tier with increasing smooth maintenance and two positive heterogeneous net-payment rates, a small customer-neutral time–regime perturbation preserves customer payments, fill, and physical cost while lowering maintenance to first order. Its adjustment cost is second order. The proof gives an explicit gain bound and a neighborhood of primitives preserving the strict improvement. It does not require translated demand or a lottery, and does not assert large gains for arbitrary commitments.

**Location:** customer-neutral direction theorem; accepted robustness subsection; complete EC robustness table; `robustness.py`.

## 4. Customer commitment is relaxed through continuation participation

**Comments 8 and 19.5.** We retain the auditable, observable-service interpretation but do not substitute an asserted field application for evidence. The revised model allows the customer to leave at every review. The outside option is the remaining service utility of the same static contract; the tariff is unchanged, and no compensating side payment is introduced.

Every reachable regime history has its own continuation-payment inequality. There are **3,280** such decision nodes in the original eight-review tree. The objective is a strictly concave quadratic form. We solve its dual numerically to propose a policy, then round and slightly contract the tier vector so that all continuation inequalities hold exactly.

The resulting deterministic protocol has reward **-5.4170322098230885**, a gain of **0.4530787632236716** over the static contract. An independent rational weighted-residual calculation gives an upper gap of **6.948160845151524e-7**. The proof uses the quadratic form's lower bound by maintenance times discounted occupation weights and includes both residual energy and dual slack. The stored rational actions and nonnegative subtree multipliers are independently replayed without solving the optimization again.

The exit option costs approximately 0.18358651 relative to full customer commitment but does not remove the gain. The extension permits history dependence; we do not assume that the original three-coordinate Markov state remains sufficient. Private information and hidden action remain distinct questions.

**Location:** customer-exit subsection; EC continuation-certificate theorem; `interim_participation.py` and `verify.py`.

## 5. Certification is strengthened on the accepted continuous-action problem

**Comments 6, 11, 16, 18.9, and 19.7.** The old finite-chain worst-case budgets were formally valid but economically loose. We preserve them as historical results; they are not the revised paper's principal learning certificate.

For the accepted scalar quadratic problem, we derive an exact policy-loss identity: discounted squared action deviations from the verified dual-optimal policy, weighted by the Bellman curvature, plus the customer's nonnegative payment slack times its multiplier. The expectation is under the deployed policy. This is a continuous-action identity, not a finite-state residual scan.

For the coupled accepted update, a residual bound uses the coercivity of the quadratic operating objective. Any feasible deployed tier vector and nonnegative customer multiplier give an upper bound consisting of residual norm squared divided by four times the coercivity constant, plus multiplier-weighted acceptance slack. The bound does not require that the proposed policy or the box-constrained optimum be interior.

The submitted portfolios are rounded to rational tiers. Symmetry, off-diagonal signs, and row sums certify the graph matrix's coercivity; Python integer arithmetic then evaluates every residual and slack exactly. The largest learned value–gradient bound at 1,024 services is **8.043576354034583e-7 per service**, while the smallest realized gain over the optimized uniform tier is above **0.016067 per service**. These budgets are well below the economic effect. They certify each submitted instance, not a distribution-free guarantee on all future graphs or a general HJB continuum theorem.

**Location:** accepted policy-loss identity and deployment-bound theorem; `portfolio_learning.py`, `verify.py`.

## 6. The learning study is coupled, larger, and equal in raw oracle information

**Comments 5, 7, 11, 12, 16, 18.7, 18.8, and 19.7.** The new study uses a portfolio of graph-coupled service tiers with a common customer's payment budget and a nonseparable alignment cost. It scales to **1,024 persistent tier coordinates**. This is an accepted review/terminal Bellman update; it is not advertised as a solution of an arbitrary 1,024-dimensional eight-stage control problem.

The critic is a scalar quadratic graph-filter network with fixed degree-twelve message-passing features and thirteen fitted readout coefficients. Its value and gradient are compatible by construction. Polynomial graph filtering and least-squares fitting are established methods, and the revised text says so. The trained coefficient vector transfers from graphs of size 32 or 64 to new graph structures at sizes 64, 256, and 1,024, without retraining.

Ten predeclared training seeds each receive 96 graphs and three scalar operational-value queries per graph. Both methods receive all **288 raw scalar observations**. The value–gradient objective adds centered directional differences formed from those very same observations; it receives no extra neighboring teacher values. Because the oracle and critic are quadratic in the inherited state, the centered directional derivative is exact in real arithmetic. The same objective is therefore also a finite-difference regularizer, not another independent baseline. Steps 0.01, 0.025, 0.05, and 0.1 are covered by the main run and sensitivity records. This new experiment has no teacher spatial grid whose numerical derivatives could be silently treated as exact continuum labels.

We retain dense Cholesky, sparse direct solution, conjugate gradients, and an analytic degree-twelve Chebyshev filter. At 1,024 coordinates, recorded median solve-and-calibration times are about 1.21 ms for the learned value–gradient actor, 7.62 ms for sparse direct, 18.01 ms for dense Cholesky, and 1.52 ms for conjugate gradients. The latter is more accurate. Offline training, graph construction, and rational audit are excluded from those deployment timings and disclosed separately; this is not a claim of faster end-to-end proof generation.

The gradient-weighted fit has a modest lower average loss but improves six of ten training-seed means, and all paired seed confidence intervals include zero. The unit for uncertainty is a training seed averaged over the four fixed test graphs, not forty independent replications. The conclusion is certified accepted deployment with a measured representation tradeoff, not universal or statistically established superiority of gradient weighting.

**Location:** accepted-learning section; main portfolio table; EC all-size, seed, and differentiation diagnostics. All 288 decisions were independently checked locally; the reproduction command generates their complete rational records.

## 7. The structural results separate new applications from standard machinery

**Comments 4, 9, 10, 18.2–18.6, and 19.6.** We add Henig, Gerchak, Ernst, and Pyke (1997) and Nasser and Turcic (2019) and compare their institutions directly with the fixed-tariff, public-state model. The former concerns inventory embedded in supply-contract design; the latter concerns temporary adjustment with private forecasts and information rents. Neither is dismissed because it has different assumptions.

The contribution discussion and EC novelty map distinguish the contract-specific results from their tools. Topkis selection, convex hull construction, occupation measures, Lagrangian duality, Riccati algebra, Sobolev fitting, and polynomial graph filtering are not claimed as inventions. The new claims concern physical-policy identification by accepted commitments, the exact accepted information decomposition, a customer-neutral improvement direction, a continuation-participation comparison, and an acceptance-aware deployment audit.

We preserve the second-order tier-grid theorem without applying it to stock discretization, constrained menus, or the continuous-rate model. Static optimization now explicitly permits upper-envelope preprocessing: crossings never attaining a stock upper envelope need not be enumerated. The Bregman result retains its multidimensional geometry caveat.

The new multi-tier inaction calculation compares a retained tier against every competing tier. It supplies complete lower and upper one-review premium-shift bounds. Under discrete concavity on a uniform menu, adjacent slopes give ordered bands and a 2-lambda-times-spacing overlap. Without that concavity, empty bands remain possible. The canonical scan reports 240 nonempty bands among 264 period–regime–tier combinations, with 24 retaining the inherited tier at the recorded premium. This is a one-review diagnostic with continuation held fixed, not a permanent-parameter comparative static.
