\section{R24 diagnostic protocol and complete numerical records}\label{cs:r24-details}
This computational supplement preserves every earlier empirical table and
execution detail relocated from the electronic companion. It also supplies the
new interior-comparator and repeated-query diagnostics. It is a code-and-data
supplement, not a substitute for a proof required to read the main paper.
All predecessor formal statements and proofs remain in the main manuscript or
electronic companion. Sources and records retain their original revision labels;
old data are not pooled into new sampling claims.

\subsection{Interior-model comparator brackets}
The coefficient seed is 2402301. For every original context and radius, two
three-coordinate integer vectors are sampled uniformly from minus seven through
seven and divided by eight. Every vector is strictly interior to the cube.
Both comparators use the true interior reward and friction. The true restricted
class uses that model's own participation and capacity coefficients; the outer
class is exactly the original analytically containing class. Both retain root,
box, and time-only amendment restrictions.

Each solver supplies a proposal and dual prices. A separate rational procedure
rounds date/service amplitudes, reconstructs root payments, and mixes within the
time-only subspace toward a strict accepted anchor. A general rational Fenchel
calculation then checks the two comparator intervals without trusting solver
status or its printed objective. Negative controls perturb root payment,
time-only equality, inequality prices, switching tensions, and interpolation
weights. The records include each rational implementation and all dual prices.
The mean, median, quartiles, ninety-fifth percentile, and maximum of total,
outer-set, and interpolation slack are retained at every radius. This is a
sensitivity diagnostic, not a statistically calibrated uncertainty model.

\subsection{Timing, training, and memory accounting}
Training seeds are 2402401 through 2402403; test seeds are 2403401 through
2403403. Each configuration has one fixed fit and a fresh held-out sample.
The predictors retain the earlier 192-feature tanh-gradient construction and
cubic radial-basis direct-price construction. Audited optimizer-generated labels contain
both the optimized value and its resource statistic; they are not information
learned without an optimizer. The entire label generation, including the
teacher workspace and rational audit, is charged to either learned method.
Shared labels are not charged twice when the two methods are jointly fitted,
but a standalone deployment of either method carries the full label bill.

A setup timer includes sparse matrix construction and workspace initialization.
Online timers include every candidate-specific operation, including numerical
repair and exact checking. Method order is independently randomized within a
context. The classical comparator retains both primal and dual continuation
state along the same ordered context sequence; each new repetition resets that
sequence. No method is allowed to use another method's fallback state. When
fallback occurs it starts in a new lifted workspace. Consequently the cost
record directly distinguishes proposal overhead from fallback avoidance and
from hypothetical warm-start savings not implemented here.

A context is the resampling unit for paired intervals. Its three timings are
averaged first, and the 32 context-level paired savings are then resampled
10,000 times with seed 2405401. These exploratory percentile intervals concern
fixed fitted policies on the declared context distribution. Repetition does
not turn 32 contexts into 96 independent training/deployment experiments.
The retrospective R22 scaling reanalysis has only eight context-level pairs
per configuration and is labeled accordingly.

Memory statistics distinguish serialized predictor bytes, reachable NumPy
array storage, and sparse input-matrix bytes. Array views may share backing
storage; sparse matrix counts exclude factorization workspace. Peak resident
memory is a whole-process high-water mark, not a per-method peak. These
quantities are reported with their scopes rather than summed into an
unsupported total-memory comparison.

\subsection{Recorded robust certification cost}
The original robust run stores each method's optimization, rational repair,
and vertex-evaluation time and the shared eight-comparator batch time. The
retrospective analysis adds that entire batch to each method when interpreting
it as an independently certified decision. It also reports the batch
separately so that actual reuse across candidate methods is visible. Original
ensemble prediction occurred before those timers; no new-machine measurement
is spliced into the archived totals. The resulting archived sum is a disclosed
lower bound on full learned end-to-end time, not evidence for acceleration.
A pointwise gain certificate is an operationally more expensive object than a
feasible proposal or a certificate used only in offline evaluation.



The fresh robust-cost check uses seed 2407401, sixteen contexts, and radii zero
and one quarter. Ensemble prediction is timed on each query rather than
precomputed outside the timer. Each of the eight comparator solves uses a
fresh workspace and an independent rational upper certificate. Candidate
ordering rotates by context. Model deserialization and geometry construction
are one-time costs and are reported separately. These measurements are made
sequentially after the nominal matched study, on the same execution host;
they are not added to the historical R23 machine times. All rational records
are independently replayed.

\section{R22 recorded objects and independent verification}
Each timed record contains the initial repaired proposal and its dual certificate, the fixed-primal polished certificate, and the actually returned post-fallback certificate. Replay verifies identical proposal coordinates, switching tensions and inequality prices before and after polishing; nonincreasing rational upper bounds; and the requested final gap. Component checks evaluate the exact four-term identity, including its outward-rounding remainder. An outside gate, when active, is recomputed from the exact proposal value; an ungated component identity is not relabeled as the selected outside decision's gap.

Validation records additionally retain the optimized restricted candidate and its full restricted dual, both deterministic ensemble outputs, reference decisions, and every corner bound. The sample observations use the selected value minus the restricted upper bound. Both the Hoeffding target and its width are independently reconstructed from these objects. The complete dual-repair checks independently optimize 225 small accepted problems, including 45 with no strict-feasibility point; they verify 3,600 exact projected-gradient steps and 450 reward/friction certificate transports. They do not call the numerical optimizer or the main certificate implementation. These are theorem checks, not speed comparisons. The 360 nonsmooth zero-curvature identities and 234 exact regularization-bias checks supplement, rather than replace, the proofs. Historical rational replay remains executable without any numerical solver. Source hashes and final-manuscript hashes are verified on the published commit, rather than only on its pre-publication workflow input.

\section{Repeated learning, difficult geometry, and complete deployment cost}\label{sec:r19-experiment}
The empirical design tests the link in Section~\ref{sec:r19-bridge} rather than using the star's easy scalar solve as evidence for difficult stochastic control. We retain the entire earlier information hierarchy, verified-cell studies, star experiments, and adverse nonlinear-transfer results in the companion and the immutable predecessor documents. The current multistage observations were produced in the R16 research run and are integrated into the manuscript here for the first time. Our fleet inference is a retrospective reanalysis of those observations. The accuracy-targeted cost study and deliberate boundary checks are new follow-up computations, separately identified in their execution records.

\subsection{A multistage, vector, nonsmooth study}
The fixed primitive has 63 nodes on a binary tree with six review levels, two service coordinates per node, six coupled quadratic resources, and four additional cross-service capacity constraints. Outside tiers vary by node and service. Every nonroot subtree retains its payment cap, and each service has its root payment equality. Absolute switching costs include installation and have endogenous signs. The displacement formulation is exactly the quadratic instance following Theorem~\ref{thm:r19-inexact}; it is not a collection of independent stars. Each context changes six resource-reward coordinates and a strictly positive friction coefficient. The complete rational primitive and context grids are recorded in the companion.

We evaluate eight independently seeded training/deployment pairs. Each pair uses 256 training contexts and 256 fresh deployment contexts. No best seed or model is selected. All seven methods use the same exact-solver label population: tanh random-feature scalar critics fitted with value-only or value-and-gradient losses; a direct-price tanh regressor; the corresponding three quadratic response surfaces; and a cubic radial-basis direct-price interpolant. The hidden tanh weights are fixed random features, not a fully trained deep network. Value-only and derivative-supervised critics both deploy through their derivative. Direct-price methods predict the same six-dimensional operational statistic. Thus the comparison does not grant neural methods a different response operator or a more favorable certificate.

The feasible outside protocol is specified and node-dependent, not proved to be the reoptimized best static protocol for this heterogeneous primitive. This study tests accepted implementation relative to that announced comparator. The separate canonical and centered-friction studies retain their reoptimized static comparators. Monetary values in the current study are total normalized tree-objective units; they are not per-review gains from the earlier star normalization. The primitive is synthetic and not calibrated to a service provider. Those distinctions prevent a gain against one known feasible protocol from being advertised as an empirical universal value of adaptation.

The R16 solver amendment increased the iteration ceiling after a failure in the R15 execution, while retaining seeds, methods, loss weights, and tolerances; the full amended study was rerun. We describe this as a frozen, independently deployed internal experiment, not an externally preregistered study. Neither this reanalysis nor the follow-up computations modify its trained models or replace its unfavorable observations.

\section{Current study: primitives, audits, and reproducibility}\label{ec:r19-details}
\subsection{Primitive construction and the scope of the experiment}
The 63-node binary tree has two service coordinates at every node. An unconditional coefficient for service $a$ at node $j$ is $c_{ja}=t_{ja}2^{5-\operatorname{depth}(j)}$, with integer $t_{ja}\in\{8,\ldots,12\}$. Outside tiers have denominator 32 and numerators from 13 through 19. Diagonal curvatures have numerators 8 through 24 and denominator 16; marginal reward offsets have numerators $-8$ through 8 and denominator 32. The six-by-126 resource map has integer entries $-4$ through 4 divided by 32. Four nonnegative cross-service capacity rows have integer coefficients 0 through 4 and right-hand sides with denominator 8. All initial installed tiers are one half. These values are generated once using primitive seed 15001; the resulting exact arrays, not a verbal generator alone, are supplied.

Every nonroot subtree has the outside protocol's payment cap separately for each service, while each service's root payment is fixed. The displacement vector therefore obeys homogeneous continuation inequalities and two root equalities. The outside protocol is heterogeneous and is not asserted to solve a static optimization problem for this primitive. Context reward numerators are independent integers from $-16$ through 16 divided by 16, and the positive friction numerator is from 2 through 32 divided by 64. The coefficient of each absolute switching difference has a numerator from 4 through 8 and denominator 8. Primitive heterogeneity does not itself constitute field calibration.

The derivative in Theorem~\ref{thm:r19-global} uses the six reward coordinates, not the scaled friction coordinate. Training rescales friction to a centered unit interval; all derivative targets are resource vectors in the reward coordinates' original units. \subsection{Feasibility and finite precision}
For each service and nonroot node, negate its weighted subtree displacement and divide by its positive payment coefficient to obtain a transfer coordinate. Negative numerical transfers are truncated to zero and the rest are rounded downward on a grid with denominator $10^8$. The sparse two-entry-per-column transfer map reconstructs a rational displacement satisfying every root equality and continuation cap exactly. A common nonnegative radial contraction toward zero enforces all box and extra capacity rows. This construction works for the study's positive payment coefficients and homogeneous outside-relative commitments; it is not claimed for arbitrary signed tariffs. The general theorem permits any independently certified feasible response instead.

Tensions are clipped to their exact friction capacities; inequality prices are clipped to nonnegativity. Prices and multipliers use denominator $10^9$. The independent verifier recomputes box feasibility, subtree sums, root equalities, resource capacities, tension capacities, exact primal gain, and an outward-rounded conjugate upper bound. It uses only Python integers and rational arithmetic, with no numerical solver or matrix inverse. In particular it does not trust the proposal's active set, the repair routine's assertions, or the solver's termination flag. The gate compares exact gain with the known outside protocol only.

\subsection{A declared service-control distribution and operational units}
Consider binary scenario trees with discount factor $0.9$ and equal branch probabilities. Thus $w_n=(0.9/2)^{\operatorname{depth}(n)}$. Tiers lie in $[0,1]$, the installed tier is $1/2$, and external net-payment coefficients are $c_n=w_n[0.8+0.1(n\bmod5)]$, with nodes numbered from zero. The accepted polytope includes every nonroot continuation cap and the root payment equality. Physical service and stock are held at their identified benchmark levels; the experiment concerns the remaining tier decision, not an untested inventory-learning claim.

The incremental profit, in profit units, is
\begin{align}\label{eq:r13-data-objective}
 G_{\alpha,\beta,\lambda}(x)={}&\sum_n(g_{0n}+\alpha g_{1n}+\beta g_{2n})(x_n-\tfrac12)
             -\sum_n w_n(x_n-\tfrac12)^2\notag\\
 &-\tfrac1{10}\sum_{n\ne o}w_n(x_n-x_{\operatorname{pa}(n)})^2
             -\lambda\sum_n w_n|x_n-x_{\operatorname{pa}(n)}|,
\end{align}
where the root's predecessor is $1/2$ in the last term. Three fixed vectors describe net marginal resource offsets after the tariff; each has zero sum. Their exact rational coordinates and generating seed are recorded. Therefore the \emph{reoptimized} fixed-tier comparator is exactly $1/2$ for every context: a common tier change has zero linear gain and strictly positive quadratic cost. This balanced-shock class is different from, and does not replace, the earlier noncentered class with boundary comparators.

One profit unit is the coefficient of a squared full-tier deviation in the per-review maintenance cost. The smooth inter-review cost has coefficient $0.1$, and $\lambda$ prices an absolute unit of reconfiguration. Adding a common level cost can make all gross resource costs positive without changing decisions. Results are divided by the discounted review mass $S_0=\sum_n w_n$. Multiplying all monetary primitives by a currency conversion multiplies every profit and certificate by that conversion; no such conversion is a field calibration. For example, a gain of $0.003$ represents $0.30$ per discounted review if one profit unit is defined as $100$ currency units. No operational population is asserted to have that calibration.

The selected value-gradient policy has empirical gain \RFourteenValidationGain\ and lower bound \RFourteenValidationFloor\ per discounted review. Both policies have positive gain lower bounds, and their small gain difference is not established as significant. This confirmation concerns the declared fixed synthetic distribution, not arbitrary shift or new primitive families. It does demonstrate positive accepted neural deployment without the classical rescue needed by the earlier poor actors.

\subsection{Structural cross-checks and preservation of adverse evidence}
The direct threshold agrees with 160 general tension linear programs. Across 480 solver comparisons, safeguarded Newton, Brent, bisection, and the breakpoint scan agree; 128 small-instance SLSQP comparisons have maximum value discrepancy \RFourteenDenseError. A further 800 arbitrary-price checks include 106 root-capacity-binding cases and verify both global bounds and their underlying remainder identity. These are implementation tests, not substitutes for the proofs. Timing the explicit threshold and scalar solvers through 32,767 leaves confirms the intended sparse implementation; the raw measurements and their two-date limitation appear in Table~\ref{tab:r14-scaling}.

A separate standard-library verifier reconstructs \RFourteenCertificates\ rational policy certificates and \RFourteenChecks\ exact algebraic checks, including every continuation cap, root equality, absolute switching identity, profit, and conjugate upper bound. The full nonlinear and boundary-inclusive predecessor experiment, its badly shifted actors, unresolved derivative comparison, and slower complete learned pipelines remain unchanged. The new result identifies a tractable institution and learning statistic under which the original integrated research objective succeeds; it does not erase failure in the broader distributions.

\section{Implementation details for verified continuation cells}\label{ec:r13-implementation}
All essential new proofs are in the main paper. This section specifies the algorithms sufficiently to reproduce their numerical implementation. The ordinary Python sources contain no service calls and use pinned NumPy and SciPy versions. Training, validation, confirmation, structural tests, and matched deployment use separate recorded seeds. The confirmation stage does not change the frozen trained model.

The analytical sparse bound remains in Electronic Companion Section~\ref{ec:r24-sparse}.
\subsection{Cell recovery and rejection}
Training starts with an explicit epigraph quadratic program. A candidate active set is only a proposal. Pivoted factorization removes redundant equality rows, and a dual feasibility linear program recovers nonnegative inequality prices and capacity-feasible switching tensions. Positive inequality prices and unsaturated zero-edge tensions supply a candidate independent basis. Saturated zero-edge tensions may instead be represented by a signed cell. Every resulting affine map is checked against all primal and dual region inequalities. Failure to find a verified basis is recorded rather than assigned a fabricated label.

The input coordinates are scaled by $(1,1,10)$. The random hidden weights are standard normal; biases are uniform on $[-1,1]$. Features concatenate a constant, the three scaled inputs, and 192 ReLU outputs. The multiclass readout is fitted by ridge least squares with coefficient $0.01$ against one-hot cell labels. All random features and coefficients are serialized. The neural policy checks its ordered first one or three cells. The nearest-anchor comparator uses Euclidean distance in the same scaled context coordinates and then checks that anchor's cell. Sequential lookup tests the same cells in their fixed training order. It receives no different library or optimal query-time face.

Numerical membership permits a $2\times10^{-9}$ inequality tolerance solely as a proposal filter. The deployed flow is rounded to denominator $10^9$, negative flow coordinates are set to zero, and a common radial scaling enforces both tier bounds. Exact subtree feasibility survives this scaling. Nonnegative prices and capacity-feasible tensions are then rounded/clipped, and Theorem~\ref{thm:r13-leakage} is evaluated in rational arithmetic. Thus the numerical region tolerance is not the final feasibility or optimality tolerance. Wrong switching signs contribute leakage, including when rounding opens a nominally fused edge.

\subsection{What each timing and face statistic measures}
The matched test measures complete pipelines, including exact audit and any classical rescue. Rescue uses a cold feasible start; a rejected region is not credited with unexecuted warm-start savings. The data separate its refinement iterations from initial cell verification. Distance is measured from the first affine proposal to the separately certified reference policy. Binding-constraint masks and switching signs use a $10^{-6}$ diagnostic tolerance and are not proofs that a mathematically active face has been identified. The independent verifier instead checks their associated repaired policies and all prices. The first sequentially tested cell is fixed, so its first-face accuracy has a different interpretation from the learned first proposal.

Offline amortization divides library-construction plus fitting time by the observed mean cold-minus-neural online time, when positive. It excludes no observed online audit cost, but it does not include hypothetical future library maintenance. It also does not turn one timing sample into a confidence statement. The complete matched records allow recalculation under a different deployment volume or hardware cost.

\subsection{Independent sampling and range reconstruction}
Training uses seed 13031 and random features seed 13032. The first validation uses 13033 and matched deployment 13034. After the deterministic corner range is saved, confirmation uses 13035. Within each cohort the two forcing coordinates and friction are independently generated from the specified finite uniform grids. Samples are not resampled when routing fails: the prescribed static policy is retained, and its certificate contributes to the mean. The first broad-range cohort remains in full. The second range is derived from the model's four forcing vertices, not from truncating unfavorable sample values. For each vertex the standard-library verifier checks both its policy and its upper bound before accepting the range.

The final confidence statement is conditional on the frozen model, distribution, and monetary normalization. Its positive gain floor compares with the static outside arrangement. The simultaneous regret ceilings are intentionally reported alongside it: they remain much wider than high-accuracy deployment tolerances. This distinction prevents the validation theorem from being described as a blanket certification of neural training, distribution shift, or superiority over classical control.

\section{Reconstruction and execution details for the scalar-critic study}\label{ec:r14-details}
\subsection{Primitive service instance and comparator}
Let $m=127$, $w=9/(10m)$, $k_i\in\{8,\ldots,12\}$, $u_i\in\{-28,\ldots,4\}$, and $v_i\in\{-20,\ldots,20\}$. NumPy's default generator with seed 14001 draws these three integer arrays in that order. They are also stored explicitly, so replay does not depend on a generator implementation. Put $\rho_i=9k_i/(80m)$, $A=-w\sum_i u_i/20$, $B=-w\sum_i v_i/20$, and $r=\sum_i\rho_i$. Smooth reward gradients at the common outside tier are
\[
 g_0=A+\alpha B+\frac{hr}{1+r},\qquad
 g_i=\frac{w(u_i+\alpha v_i)}{20}-\frac{h\rho_i}{1+r}.
\]
Their sum is exactly zero, and $\rho_i g_0-g_i=\rho_i A-wu_i/20+\alpha(\rho_i B-wv_i/20)+h\rho_i$. A common tier displacement therefore has zero first-order reward and strictly positive quadratic cost; installation friction reinforces that inequality. Thus $1/2$ is the reoptimized static comparator, not an arbitrary fixed baseline. The smooth node reward is $g_i(x_i-1/2)-\chi_i(x_i-1/2)^2/2$ relative to that baseline. Given fixed tariff $c_i$, take its resource increment to be $c_i(x_i-1/2)$ minus this reward. Adding a sufficiently large constant makes all gross resource costs nonnegative on the compact tier interval without changing any decision. This is the identified-stock, quadratic-resource specialization of the unified model, not an empirical fit of demand or a claim that every inventory cost reduces to a quadratic.

\subsection{Neural loss, actual computation, and reproducibility}
Write $z=(h,\alpha,(\lambda-0.105)/0.095)$ and
$\widehat J_\omega(z)=a+b^\top z+\sum_{j=1}^{192}c_j\tanh(W_j^\top z+\zeta_j)$.
The hidden coefficients are fixed normal draws with standard deviation $1.5$, and biases are uniform on $[-1,1]$. Only $a,b,c$ are fitted. The derivative of this same scalar function, not an independent vector actor, produces the coupling price. Value and value-gradient fits minimize the sample mean of $(\widehat J-J)^2+\gamma(\partial_h\widehat J-v^*)^2$ plus $10^{-9}$ times the squared readout norm, with $\gamma=0$ and $0.25$, respectively. The analytical derivative is used in training and deployment. Targets use the structure-aware scalar solve; small-instance independent optimization and exact gap replay check the solver. In finite precision target optimality is established to the recorded numerical tolerances, not as a symbolic expression for every training label.

The paired seed interval in the paper is a Student interval on eight seed-level mean differences. It is a conventional small-sample approximation, not a distribution-free guarantee. The independent gain bound is different: its bounded-variable argument is conditional on the frozen models and uses no Gaussian approximation. Only the primary paired regret endpoint was designated before fitting; secondary comparisons are descriptive and do not receive unadjusted claims of significance.

The total label-generation time, charged once per shared training sample rather than twice for the paired methods, is \RFourteenLabelTime\ seconds; fitting the eight value-only and eight derivative-weighted readouts takes \RFourteenValueFit\ and \RFourteenGradientFit\ seconds, respectively. These offline costs are not included in per-query timings and are not claimed to amortize into a speed advantage. Raw deployment timings cover 32 fixed test contexts, three repetitions, and a rotated method order. The exact audit is performed for every proposed policy and again after any refinement. Components and total times are retained for every repetition, including immediate policies. No failed proposal or timing row is dropped.

\subsection{Independent certificates and exact fallback semantics}
The proposer rounds each leaf transfer down to a multiple of $10^{-9}$, clips to its interval, and rescales by an exact common rational factor if the root cap is exceeded. This preserves all leaf continuation inequalities and the root equality by construction. It rounds a proposed total dual price to a rational number and evaluates every conjugate directly. Such a price need not satisfy approximate stationarity: weak duality alone validates the upper bound. The independent verifier imports only the Python standard library, reconstructs all primitive coefficients from the stored integers, and checks exact rational equalities, inequalities, absolute switching, profit, and upper bounds. Numerical gradient inequalities and wall-clock times are explicitly outside that rational proof checker.

The validation policy performs no full objective solve after prediction. The capacity-only response can require scalar water filling, which is part of feasibility implementation and is not hidden as a free oracle. The matched-accuracy policy is a different, explicitly labeled pipeline: a certificate gap above $10^{-7}$ invokes the full scalar solver and a second audit. Its last policy is feasible and its exact gap meets that tolerance. The raw-policy table and independent expected-gain guarantee never substitute this refined policy for the learned proposal.
