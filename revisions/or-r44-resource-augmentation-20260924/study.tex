\section{Recovery Guarantees and Their Computational Cost}\label{sec:r44-study}
The new study tests the constructive guarantee, including the case where the original price gap is nonzero. It complements, rather than replaces, the preceding prefix, continuous-enumeration, and independently formulated nonlinear studies. All new arithmetic, feasibility residuals, charge accounting, and certificates are rational; reported decimal tables are rounded displays of the archived fractions.

The seeded regression suite compares 144 small instances with exhaustive catalog optimization. It varies one through six branches, budgets one through three, four risk regimes, heterogeneous and zero shortfall curvatures, repeated caps, endpoint promises, and zero, affine, or nonmonotone nonnegative charges. Every original-budget interval contains the exhaustive optimum and every recovered policy satisfies the stated augmentation inequality. Sixteen additional boundary cases cover a nonzero lowest catalog level, a full-catalog budget, zero curvature, and both coarse and fine requested accuracy. Fourteen semantic certificate corruptions are rejected by the independent checker. The strict-gap instance in Section~\ref{sec:r44-proof} has exact gap $3/640$; it makes the difference between a priced upper bound and an original-budget optimum observable.

Table~\ref{tab:r44-scaling} reports all twelve scaling and accuracy runs. The family has distinct caps and heterogeneous shortfall costs, reaches 256 branches and 65 catalog levels, and allows the alphabet budget to vary through eight. The table separates the original-budget interval from the recovered union payoff; comparing them as though they used the same number of symbols would be incorrect. The charged cases report the actual union-charge excess. The companion gives the complete parameter order, instrumented times, traced memory, rational bit lengths, and separate checker times. Every reported union policy satisfies its exact root promise and every branch realization bound, without numerical tolerances.

The independent checker imports neither the recovery procedure nor the fixed-book allocator. It rebuilds the inherited independent support dynamic program at the two reported prices, checks endpoint optimality, replays each installed policy directly, and verifies the weighted-target and charge identities. A feasible original-budget policy supplies the lower bound separately. Inherited R43 price, prefix, and nonquadratic-class audits, together with the R42 and R39/R33--R37 regressions, are re-executed in disposable copies. The six previously reported SCIP comparisons remain explicitly preceding evidence; they are not represented as newly run numerical experiments.
