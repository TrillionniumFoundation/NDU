\section{Global Codebook Optimization: Implementation and Verification}\label{sec:ec-r34}
This section documents Theorems~\ref{thm:r34-global} and \ref{thm:r34-pathwise}. The complete structural and algorithmic proofs appear in the article; no omitted numerical experiment is being used to supply a proof step.

\subsection{Rational implementation concordance}
The implementation accepts integers, rational strings, or exact fractions. It rejects binary floating-point inputs, unordered or repeated caps, nonpositive probabilities, probabilities not summing to one, negative intermediate curvature, and terminal parameters outside $r\geq q>0$. Sorted distinct caps are part of the interface; sorting unorganized observations would first require sorting their associated probabilities and costs together. An arbitrary-order interface would add $O(k\log k)$ rational comparisons.

The six prefix moment arrays evaluate \eqref{eq:r34-edge} and \eqref{eq:r34-tail} without summing over an interval at each query. The routine \texttt{tail\_polynomial} returns the exact coefficients $A,B,C$ of $Az^2+Bz+C$; \texttt{tail} compares all clamped cell minima and saves the attaining final level. The routine \texttt{randomized\_frontiers} implements \eqref{eq:r34-prefix} using two cost rows and retained predecessor indices, then combines them with terminal costs. A strict improvement stores a reconstructed codebook; ties can keep any incumbent. Duplicate terminal levels are deleted. Budgets greater than the number of branches repeat the zero-loss solution.

The bit bound does not require that the implementation form a common denominator. Every addition, multiplication, and division returns a reduced fraction. Comparisons are exact cross products. Moment arrays contain sums of fixed-degree input products. Edges use products of moment values and cap endpoints. Tail minimizers divide two such coefficients, with a strictly positive divisor on nonterminal cells. Prefix paths add at most $k$ edge costs, and a complete candidate adds one optimized tail. This dependency pattern rules out uncontrolled nested algebraic extensions: all candidates and the reconstructed lottery probabilities are rational. Evaluating final probabilities adds differences and a division of rational codewords and has the same polynomial height bound. The stated conservative $S$ covers both objective values and reconstructed controls, rather than only response coefficients.

For convex nondecreasing intermediate costs that are not quadratic, Lemma~\ref{lem:r34-anchor} and the prefix recurrence still apply when terminal reward is quadratic. Each terminal interval instead requires minimizing a strictly convex one-dimensional function. Thus the finite reduction persists with a one-dimensional convex minimization oracle. Exact rational attainment and the particular Turing bound are not transferred to a cost family without an exact representation and such an oracle.

\subsection{Independent checks and their logical scope}
The new verification script uses three complementary comparisons. First, it enumerates every cap-anchored prefix on small instances and every interval for the continuous highest level. This comparator does not call the production dynamic program or its moment routines. Instead, it evaluates the original branchwise lottery formula at three rational points to recover the interval quadratic, minimizes that quadratic exactly, and compares all budgets. It is exhaustive over the theorem-reduced candidate set; Lemma~\ref{lem:r34-anchor}, not enumeration alone, justifies reduction from all continuous codebooks.

Second, a separate direct evaluator tests codebooks containing noncap levels on a rational grid. Each sampled codebook has loss at least the computed frontier. These are counterexample-search tests and are not described as exhaustive continuous optimization. Third, controller replay reconstructs every branch's adjacent lottery, checks nonnegative probabilities summing to one, verifies its expected cap and the root promise exactly, and recomputes reward independently of prefix costs. It also reports which realized draws would violate pathwise participation. Deterministic solutions are separately replayed using their highest eligible codewords and checked against the pathwise equality proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:r34-pathwise}.

There are 51 small exact instances, comprising 48 seeded heterogeneous cases, the inherited equiprobable three-branch example, the off-cap example, and a one-branch zero-intermediate-curvature boundary. The tests use heterogeneous probabilities, positive rational terminal curvature, intermediate curvatures including zero, and the allowed boundary $r=q$. They perform 1,932 exhaustive candidate comparisons, 446 randomized/deterministic frontier equalities, 1,073 direct nonanchor-grid comparisons, and 1,346 direct moment equalities. Including the scale family, 255 expected-participation controllers are replayed. Ten invalid-input tests verify that unsupported numerical conventions are rejected.

The scale family has 16, 32, 64, and 128 branches and computes every budget from one through eight. Caps are $b_j=j/(k+1)$, probabilities are $\pi_j=2j/[k(k+1)]$, and intermediate curvature is $(j\bmod 5)/3$. Terminal parameters are $r=2,q=1$. These are reproducible synthetic inputs, not a service calibration. Machine-dependent elapsed times and exact losses are recorded in \texttt{scaling.csv}; no published specialized solver is being timed. The independent exhaustive comparator is appropriate for validating the global frontier, not for asserting practical superiority over specialized parametric-flow software.

\subsection{Preserved evidence}
The piecewise-quadratic response compiler and its whole-path KKT comparator are rerun in a temporary directory using their unchanged R33 scripts. Their original output files remain unmodified. The new \texttt{inherited\_verification.json} records the rerun and the script hashes. All R31--R33 theorem modules, the prior response letters, and earlier favorable and unfavorable numerical records remain on the revision branch. The source-preservation manifest checks the inherited reader modules byte for byte. The new frontier does not reinterpret a quadratic explicit-table lower bound as a lower bound against all shared representations, change the public-observation convention, extend the radial comparative static to convex order, or imply a finite-cut guarantee for continuous outer design.
