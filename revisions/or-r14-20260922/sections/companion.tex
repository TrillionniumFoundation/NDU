\subsection{Primitive service instance and comparator}
Let $m=127$, $w=9/(10m)$, $k_i\in\{8,\ldots,12\}$, $u_i\in\{-28,\ldots,4\}$, and $v_i\in\{-20,\ldots,20\}$. NumPy's default generator with seed 14001 draws these three integer arrays in that order. They are also stored explicitly, so replay does not depend on a generator implementation. Put $\rho_i=9k_i/(80m)$, $A=-w\sum_i u_i/20$, $B=-w\sum_i v_i/20$, and $r=\sum_i\rho_i$. Smooth reward gradients at the common outside tier are
\[
 g_0=A+\alpha B+\frac{hr}{1+r},\qquad
 g_i=\frac{w(u_i+\alpha v_i)}{20}-\frac{h\rho_i}{1+r}.
\]
Their sum is exactly zero, and $\rho_i g_0-g_i=\rho_i A-wu_i/20+\alpha(\rho_i B-wv_i/20)+h\rho_i$. A common tier displacement therefore has zero first-order reward and strictly positive quadratic cost; installation friction reinforces that inequality. Thus $1/2$ is the reoptimized static comparator, not an arbitrary fixed baseline. The smooth node reward is $g_i(x_i-1/2)-\chi_i(x_i-1/2)^2/2$ relative to that baseline. Given fixed tariff $c_i$, take its resource increment to be $c_i(x_i-1/2)$ minus this reward. Adding a sufficiently large constant makes all gross resource costs nonnegative on the compact tier interval without changing any decision. This is the identified-stock, quadratic-resource specialization of the unified model, not an empirical fit of demand or a claim that every inventory cost reduces to a quadratic.

\subsection{Neural loss, actual computation, and reproducibility}
Write $z=(h,\alpha,(\lambda-0.105)/0.095)$ and
$\widehat J_\omega(z)=a+b^\top z+\sum_{j=1}^{192}c_j\tanh(W_j^\top z+\zeta_j)$.
The hidden coefficients are fixed normal draws with standard deviation $1.5$, and biases are uniform on $[-1,1]$. Only $a,b,c$ are fitted. The derivative of this same scalar function, not an independent vector actor, produces the coupling price. Value and value-gradient fits minimize the sample mean of $(\widehat J-J)^2+\gamma(\partial_h\widehat J-v^*)^2$ plus $10^{-9}$ times the squared readout norm, with $\gamma=0$ and $0.25$, respectively. The analytical derivative is used in training and deployment. Targets use the structure-aware scalar solve; small-instance independent optimization and exact gap replay check the solver. In finite precision target optimality is established to the recorded numerical tolerances, not as a symbolic expression for every training label.

The paired seed interval in the paper is a Student interval on eight seed-level mean differences. It is a conventional small-sample approximation, not a distribution-free guarantee. The independent gain bound is different: its bounded-variable argument is conditional on the frozen models and uses no Gaussian approximation. Only the primary paired regret endpoint was designated before fitting; secondary comparisons are descriptive and do not receive unadjusted claims of significance.

The total label-generation time, charged once per shared training sample rather than twice for the paired methods, is \RFourteenLabelTime\ seconds; fitting the eight value-only and eight derivative-weighted readouts takes \RFourteenValueFit\ and \RFourteenGradientFit\ seconds, respectively. These offline costs are not included in per-query timings and are not claimed to amortize into a speed advantage. Raw deployment timings cover 32 fixed test contexts, three repetitions, and a rotated method order. The exact audit is performed for every proposed policy and again after any refinement. Components and total times are retained for every repetition, including immediate policies. No failed proposal or timing row is dropped.

\subsection{Independent certificates and exact fallback semantics}
The proposer rounds each leaf transfer down to a multiple of $10^{-9}$, clips to its interval, and rescales by an exact common rational factor if the root cap is exceeded. This preserves all leaf continuation inequalities and the root equality by construction. It rounds a proposed total dual price to a rational number and evaluates every conjugate directly. Such a price need not satisfy approximate stationarity: weak duality alone validates the upper bound. The independent verifier imports only the Python standard library, reconstructs all primitive coefficients from the stored integers, and checks exact rational equalities, inequalities, absolute switching, profit, and upper bounds. Numerical gradient inequalities and wall-clock times are explicitly outside that rational proof checker.

The validation policy performs no full objective solve after prediction. The capacity-only response can require scalar water filling, which is part of feasibility implementation and is not hidden as a free oracle. The matched-accuracy policy is a different, explicitly labeled pipeline: a certificate gap above $10^{-7}$ invokes the full scalar solver and a second audit. Its last policy is feasible and its exact gap meets that tolerance. The raw-policy table and independent expected-gain guarantee never substitute this refined policy for the learned proposal.
