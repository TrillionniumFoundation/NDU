\vspace{.3in}{Anonymous manuscript for Operations Research\par}\vspace{.25in}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\textbf{Abstract.}
Can an externally priced service protocol adapt without reducing customer utility or service? We study persistent tiers, physical stock, costly reconfiguration, and participation at every review. A full-tree balance characterization relates accepted adaptation to continuation prices, boundary scarcity, and switching capacities. On scalar trees, exact payment-transfer coordinates yield a sparse critical-friction program, a two-pass dual repair, and a constructive profitable policy when friction is below a separating threshold. A computable policy-loss bound penalizes inaccurate prices and switching faces without requiring the unknown optimal face. For quadratic resources, verified parameter cells support compatible scalar value experts and neural routing with explicit rejection and refinement. A model-derived validation range gives simultaneous expected-gain and regret guarantees for frozen complete pipelines. Nonzero-switching experiments exercise the structural theory, while matched-accuracy comparisons include classical cell lookup, exact audits, offline construction, and actual fallback. Independent validation establishes positive expected gain over static in the declared synthetic distribution, not universal neural superiority. Earlier information-value results and unfavorable nonlinear transfer evidence are retained. The results connect service acceptance, implementable learning, and policy certification without conflating these guarantees.

\par\vspace{.10in}\textbf{Area of review:} Stochastic Models.

\section{Introduction}
A service provider can revise both physical capacity and the service tier attached to it. A higher tier earns a premium but can also increase indemnity and maintenance costs; replacing an installed tier consumes resources. Adaptation is useful only when these gains survive the customer's commitments and comparison with a properly optimized outside protocol. We ask when an accepted adaptive protocol creates value, how switching friction changes that answer, and how a learned implementation can be evaluated under the same acceptance requirements.

We study Neural Differential Utility as joint control of physical activity and a persistent, externally priced contractual state. Regimes, stock, tiers, and realized fulfillment are observable and contractible. The tariff and accounting rule are fixed before optimization. The provider commits to an announced contingent protocol; in the continuation model the customer can leave at every review. There is no private type or hidden effort. The model is an ordinary Markov decision process with expanded state and action spaces, not a claimed escape from stochastic control. Its distinctive comparison preserves the customer's utility, expected fill, and physical resource commitments relative to an optimized static arrangement.

Our first contribution identifies the structure of accepted adaptation on a full scenario tree. With absolute switching costs, marginal operating rewards must be balanced by continuation prices, boundary scarcity, and bounded switching tensions. On a scalar tree, eliminating the tensions yields cumulative subtree cuts and an exact critical-friction program. This extends the earlier local edge criterion beyond a two-review adjustment. The general balance theorem admits vector tiers, joint smooth graph costs, multiple commitments, signed net-payment rates, boundary solutions, and nonconstant outside protocols. With all temporal and installation edges penalized, a constant protocol with no initial mismatch becomes optimal at sufficiently large friction. For an already-changing outside protocol, the optimality region can instead have an upper endpoint. We give both a complete characterization and a constructive improving policy when the balance fails. The price--capacity margin bounds accepted value; exact equality requires the stated quadratic curvature and feasible-step clearance, rather than holding for arbitrary boundary problems.

Our second contribution makes the structure algorithmic. On a scalar tree, nonnegative payment transfers parameterize the entire accepted polytope. A two-pass tension repair gives an explicit upper bound on critical friction, while a separating transfer provides both a lower bound and a profitable accepted decision. Keeping the incidence factors separate prevents quadratic fill-in on high-branching trees. Sparse linear programming and a bracketed first-order method are evaluated separately; linear work per iteration does not imply a linear-time optimization algorithm.

Our third contribution supplies an implementable connection to learning. An approximate-face certificate uses only a candidate and predicted prices; wrong switching signs and slack commitments incur explicit loss penalties. Under quadratic resources, offline price maps define verifiable parameter cells and compatible scalar value experts. A neural router proposes cells, but feasibility, price signs, and an exact policy audit determine acceptance. This removes the unknown query-time face from the sufficient condition. Rejection and actual refinement remain part of the complete algorithm, and classical lookup of the same cells remains a comparator.

Our fourth contribution executes the theory and validation. Nonzero-switching experiments trace accepted decisions through critical friction. Independent samples evaluate frozen neural and nearest-anchor pipelines, with a model-derived range and a positive expected-gain bound relative to static. Matched-accuracy tests include the entire deployment and offline cost. The exact canonical information hierarchy retains its total accepted improvement of 0.63666527 and inherited-tier contribution of 0.01929038. The earlier noncentered boundary experiments and severe neural transfer failures remain intact in the companion. No field calibration, general distribution-shift guarantee, or universal gradient-supervision advantage is inferred.

\subsection{Related literature and the incremental contribution}
Inventory decisions and service contracts have long been studied jointly. \citet{Henig1997} embed inventory decisions in supply-contract design; \citet{LiangAtkins2013,Sieke2012,Katok2008} study agreements, penalties, coordination, and review periods. Dynamic stocking under service-level agreements is established \citep{Hosseinifard2022,Caggiano2007,Abbasi2022}. Here the static-contract comparator can already adapt physical stock to the regime. The additional decisions are accepted revisions of contractual tiers, not state-aware inventory actions withheld from the benchmark.

Private information and unobserved effort create different constraints. \citet{NasserTurcic2019} study temporary contract adjustment with a private demand forecast; \citet{PlambeckZenios2003} study incentive-efficient control with unobserved effort. We impose public-state continuation participation, not truth telling or hidden-action incentive compatibility. Combined pricing and inventory optimization is also established \citep{FedergruenHeching1999}; our tariff and demand law remain external to the provider's control.

Constrained Markov decision processes, occupation measures, and Lagrangian resource prices are classical \citep{Altman1999}. Conditional promised utility supports recursive contracting \citep{SpearSrivastava1987}, while self-enforcing wage contracts analyze participation over time \citep{ThomasWorrall1988}. Our remaining-payment state is a service-control specialization, not a new principle of dynamic programming. The contribution is the exact continuation-price and switching-capacity characterization, including its cumulative cut form and friction comparative statics, under the stated observable-service institution.

Monotone selections follow \citet{Topkis1978}, and hysteretic control has predecessors including \citet{HippHolzbaur1988}. Supporting-price stock identification, normal cones, Bregman decompositions, and strong-convexity residual bounds use standard convex tools \citep{BoydVandenberghe2004}. We state those tools explicitly so that their specialization is not mistaken for independent mathematical novelty. Approximate dynamic programming also uses optimization to connect approximate values and decisions \citep{DeFariasVanRoy2003}. Our complete-tree audit retains all participation rows and boundary prices rather than relying on a collection of unverified one-step errors.

Derivative-weighted fitting follows Sobolev training \citep{Czarnecki2017}; graph filtering follows \citet{Defferrard2016}. Safe and constrained learning studies cumulative constraints and, in some models, unknown safe regions \citep{Satija2020,WachiSui2020}. Here the feasible polyhedron is known; learning proposes a policy and a separate calculation certifies it. The acceptance-face theorem and certificate-risk selection result explain the role and limits of this separation. We benchmark SciPy's sequential quadratic programming implementation alongside the preserved sparse Newton methods \citep{Virtanen2020}; no custom implementation is declared the practical optimization frontier. Forward--backward value-process methods \citep{MaProtterYong1994,HanJentzenE2018} and Markov-chain approximation \citep{KushnerDupuis2001} support the separately preserved continuous-time material, not the finite-tree theorem by analogy.

Graph total variation and regularization paths have established dual and algorithmic representations \citep{TibshiraniTaylor2011,ArnoldTibshirani2016}. Explicit constrained control uses multiparametric quadratic programs \citep{Bemporad2002}, and safe screening uses gap-based regions \citep{Fercoq2015}. Our contribution is not to rename those methods: it is the continuation-transfer representation, constructive price repair, and their integration with verified scalar experts and independently validated accepted deployment.

The main paper contains the institution, essential proofs, canonical comparison, and current implementation results. Optional predecessor proofs and diagnostic tables are in the electronic companion. The historical supplement preserves the full earlier diffusion, continuous-time, and computational development without making it a prerequisite for understanding the current results.

\section{Conclusions and operational implications}
Accepted adaptation is a reallocation of real resources under commitments, not a transfer taken from the customer. On a tree, cumulative continuation prices and switching capacities determine which reallocations survive the exit option. The full-tree criterion extends the scalar edge test and distinguishes a zero-switching comparator's critical friction from the interval of frictions supporting an already-changing outside protocol. Boundary and multiple-budget cases belong to the same balance system.

Learning enters through a verified value--decision representation rather than an assumed optimal face. Arbitrary predicted prices produce a full-horizon certificate with explicit continuation and switching leakage. Quadratic parameter cells make compatible experts usable without solving the query problem first; a learned index is accepted only after its region and policy are checked. Independent validation establishes a positive expected gain over static for a frozen policy in the declared distribution. It does not establish near-optimal raw learning, universal transfer, or superiority over classical cell lookup. The retained nonlinear failures and the new rejection rates identify where refinement remains necessary. Calibrating a service operation, changing tariffs endogenously, and treating private information are distinct extensions, not implicit features of the present model.
