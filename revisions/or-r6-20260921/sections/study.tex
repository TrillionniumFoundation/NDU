 P=\begin{pmatrix}.70&.25&.05\\.15&.70&.15\\.05&.25&.70\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Stock is $0,0.5,\ldots,6$ and the primary tier grid is $0,0.1,\ldots,1$. The cost coefficients are $c=0.3$, $h=0.6$, $p=0.65$, $\kappa=1.2$, $m=1.5$, and $\lambda=0.6$. Premiums satisfy $r_z=0.6+1.4z$. Thus the compatibility sufficient bound is $1.4\geq1.2$. There are 264 nonterminal states and 37,752 full state--action pairs. The customer extension fixes $\nu=2$ and a common retainer of 6; these do not change any historical incremental provider payoff. Every service measure in the new comparisons is a ratio of expected discounted totals, not an average of random pathwise ratios.

\subsection{A service and participation frontier}
Table~\ref{tab:r6frontier} reports the full-stock occupation program. Requiring the static fill level raises the dynamic cost to the same physical cost as the static policy. Requiring the static customer's utility as well reduces provider reward further, but a gain of $0.625140311972$ remains against the \emph{best continuous} static tier. Adding the static physical-cost cap leaves this solution unchanged. Total provider profit after the common retainer increases from $0.129889026953$ to $0.755029338925$; customer surplus after the same retainer is unchanged. The exact certificate in Section~\ref{sec:pareto} verifies all three equalities, not just the objective difference.

More stringent service is expensive: at a $0.95$ fill floor with the same customer reservation utility, provider incremental reward is $-6.266872787932$, below the static reference, and physical cost rises. This point is feasible for the operating commitment program but fails a zero provider outside option with retainer 6. Its purpose is to show the service-price frontier, not to recommend an unaccepted agreement. Thus enlarging dynamic flexibility does not make arbitrarily demanding service commitments free.

The parameter study separately varies $\lambda,\kappa,m$, the premium slope, discounting, and regime persistence, leaving all other primitives fixed. \subsection{An operational continuous problem and actual neural training}
For the connected continuous problem use $T=8$, $Q=P-I$, $\rho=-\log(0.97)$, $\bar v=0.5$, and the same inventory and tier-reward coefficients. Refining from 20 to 160 tier intervals changes the initial chain value from $-5.193740808$ to $-5.158941235$; the intermediate values are $-5.173537813$ and $-5.163828702$. Both the mesh and the time step change, and the problem is nonmanufactured. These values are not a comparison with the original jump-adjustment optimum: the rate model has an explicit speed limit and different adjustment units.

Table~\ref{tab:r6learning} reports all four 6,000-step training runs. Adding numerical derivative targets reduces the evaluated chain-policy loss for both paired seeds, from $0.029062$ to $0.014750$ and from $0.020323$ to $0.006948$. Training costs are higher for derivative supervision; the labels contain additional information. Two seeds do not support a universal statistical superiority claim. Worst-case residual budgets remain substantially larger than observed initial losses; they are displayed rather than substituted with the smaller training errors. The complete numerical certificate scans all 98,172 evaluation points. There is no claim of a uniform continuum neural-HJB error enclosure from these finite-chain calculations.

\section{Operational implications and evidence scope}
Three decisions should be separated. First choose the counterparty's commitment and reservation utility, rather than allowing the provider to price its own success criterion. Then optimize the contingent contract against an optimized static alternative, charging the same installation cost. Finally determine the information and computational representation needed to implement that protocol. In the canonical case, regime adaptation generates most of the reward increment, inherited-tier information adds a smaller strictly positive amount, and customer-preserving adaptation remains beneficial after service degradation has been ruled out.

The mathematical identity of NDU here is explicit: externally priced joint physical and persistent-contract control, with compatible value gradients for continuous reconfiguration and independently evaluated error budgets. Its benefits are not a separation from expanded-action Markov decision processes. The structural contribution is the combination of primitive monotonicity, implementable switching thresholds, dual-coordinate envelope reduction, and an accepted-contract comparison that can be verified without relying on neural performance. The learning component supplies an actually trained derivative-based deployment rule for the connected operational model.

All earlier valid material is retained. The physical-cost oracle, payoff--kernel geometry, entropic identity, strong-concavity residual lemma, singular-volatility conditioning, stopped HJB theorem with lateral errors, manufactured-solution calculation, and historical neural diagnostics remain in the EC or their original archival paths. The identical-data least-squares result continues to report parameterization parity. These materials are not silently pooled with the new contract-learning experiment.
