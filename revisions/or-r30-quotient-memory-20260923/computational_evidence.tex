\section{Computational Evidence}\label{sec:computational}
\subsection{Comparisons, measurement, and audit scopes}
All instances are synthetic rational service agreements. The binary comparison family uses discount and payment coefficients one, two public regimes per noninitial date, independent seeded quadratic rewards, and continuation caps above a known feasible constant-tier policy. Thus an exact root promise is available without using any optimizer. The horizon ranges from four to ten dates for the unfolded comparisons, with two seeds at each horizon. A separate grid comparison extends to twelve dates. Every input and implemented policy is retained.

We implement four explicitly defined methods. The quotient compiler merges coefficient events on the public graph. The independent tree method unfolds the same input, changes variables as in Proposition~\ref{prop:laminar}, and performs occurrence-indexed exact marginal reduction without public-vertex memoization. It uses a separate interpolation implementation, not the quotient code. The generic numerical comparison applies SciPy's sparse trust-region interior-point method to the unfolded convex quadratic problem \citep{Virtanen2020}. The primal promise method performs backward dynamic programming directly on the public graph, restricting successor promises to a grid while allowing each current action to be the continuous residual. None of these baselines receives the quotient optimum as an input.

Each case--method pair runs in a fresh sequential worker. Three repetitions are retained with their minimum, median, and maximum. Numerical-library imports are outside the timed method calls and are performed in all workers. Peak memory is an absolute process high-water mark, including the common runtime and libraries; it does not estimate allocator-level incremental memory. The comparison suite repeats construction, root inversion, certificate generation, and the inherited whole-response audit separately. In the scale suite, construction, inversion, and machine minimization are repeated three times; certificate generation and an independent \emph{single-query} policy audit are each timed once and explicitly identified as diagnostics. The latter checks conditional caps, exact state flow, stationarity, complementarity, root payment, and reward without checking every affine response segment. The companion specifies all tolerances, generators, and measurement boundaries.

\subsection{Exact tree agreement and a public-graph primal alternative}
Table~\ref{tab:comparison} reports the unfoldable comparisons. All eight tree optima equal the quotient optima as exact rational numbers. The numerical convex solves terminate successfully; their largest absolute value difference is $3.64\times10^{-7}$ and their largest primal constraint residual is below $2.63\times10^{-14}$. A small residual is not an exact optimality certificate: the rational quotient/tree comparison and the separate certificate audits provide a different type of evidence.

For these implementations and inputs, the tree-to-quotient timing ratio ranges from approximately $1.9$ to $40.7$. These are comparisons with a specified occurrence-indexed baseline, not a performance bound against the best laminar solver. A normalized memoized version of the tree reduction has the same recurrence as the quotient compiler; no speed advantage over that equivalent implementation is claimed. The generic convex comparison also concerns an explicitly unfolded formulation, not every possible compact reformulation.

Table~\ref{tab:grid} provides the more important structural control: a competing algorithm that already exploits the public graph. Its policies satisfy the exact root promise and all continuation caps under rational replay. At resolution 80, the largest absolute loss among the six reported horizon--seed pairs is below $9.77\times10^{-5}$. One instance is already exact at resolution 40; others retain a positive loss. The gain from the dual representation in this study is exact continuous-response closure and an exactly certified policy without selecting a promise mesh, rather than simply avoiding history enumeration.

We do not label the promise-grid implementation as SDDP. An SDDP performance experiment would additionally specify initialization, sampling, cut selection, stopping rules, and comparable error guarantees. Counting a hypothetical scenario tree does not supply that experiment. Our public-graph grid provides a concrete state-method baseline for the stated scalar problem; results here do not rank stochastic dual decomposition implementations on larger, less structured convex models.

\subsection{Graph scale, response density, and rational precision}
Tables~\ref{tab:representation} and \ref{tab:phases} separate three different sources of cost. The broadly capped coupled family has heterogeneous payment and reward coefficients and rational transitions. Its maximum coefficient length rises from 198 to 388 to 768 bits as the horizon doubles from 16 to 32 to 64 dates. A tighter-cap 128-date instance reaches 1,501 bits. This tested growth is approximately linear in horizon; it is not asserted to establish an asymptotic growth law for arbitrary inputs.

The late-renewal family retains heterogeneous, binding caps at the penultimate date and recombines afterward. It reaches 2,045 public vertices and 8,164 edges. It has ten global response knots, 22,419 stored segments, and a largest coefficient length of 2,213 bits. The optimal protocol requires four writable symbols. Construction takes a median 2.62 seconds, while the separate certificate-generation and single-query audit measurements take 0.87 and 1.19 seconds. This is a long rational-horizon example with sparse cap activation; it is not presented as a worst-case response-density test.

The complementary dense-knot family has slack continuation caps and distinct local clipping thresholds. At 511 vertices it stores 197,369 affine segments, compared with 3,161 at 63 vertices. It exposes approximately quadratic table growth even though coefficient lengths remain small and execution needs only one symbol. The difference between this case and the late-renewal case makes the distinction between a global knot universe, stored response segments, coefficient precision, and execution memory visible in measured data. We also retain a censored exploratory tight-cap run that did not complete its full repeated protocol within the imposed run limit; it is not treated as a successful large-instance timing.

A compiled root curve supports repeated inversions without rebuilding all response tables. The companion reports 101 evenly spaced root queries for each late-renewal size. Those times include inversion only; emitting and certifying a new policy for each promise is an additional operation. The read-only response tables and their numerical precision remain charged separately from the minimized writable alphabet.

\subsection{Verification and the retained evidence boundary}
All 35 inherited exact cases reproduce their complete response curves and values, with 147,157 assertion groups in the inherited independent verifier. Forty additional small graph instances agree exactly with the independently implemented tree reduction. Two hundred and two comparisons of explicitly expanded continuation plans check the behavioral equivalence; a separate case reduces two distinct incoming prices to a single necessary symbol. Exhaustive enumeration checks the ordered frontier against 2,308 arbitrary set partitions, including unequal probabilities and nonquadratic costs. Boundary tests cover 150 feasible corridor anchors, 96 tied-normal occurrences, and 1,185 exact supporting inequalities. The test suite also rejects deliberately invalid inputs and altered certificates. These are implementation and finite-instance checks, not independent mathematical proofs of universal claims.

The earlier deployment study is neither removed nor used as favorable algorithmic evidence. At the strict tolerance, 83 of 84 cache queries refreshed and no cache speed advantage was measured. Switching paths, comparator-adjusted rents, continuous-state envelopes, correlated certificates, and governance results retain their original statements, assumptions, proofs, and numerical records in the supplementary theory volumes. The new price compiler and the unfavorable cache experiment test different algorithms and different questions.
