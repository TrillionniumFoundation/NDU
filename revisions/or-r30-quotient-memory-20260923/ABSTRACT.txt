A service provider can return to the same public operating state with different obligations inherited from past renewal reviews. We characterize when these obligations admit an exact finite-memory implementation. For positive additive payments and separable quadratic rewards without switching costs, the full-history problem is a classical laminar allocation problem. We prove an explicit occurrence-to-vertex quotient and show that normalized memoization yields a globally bounded family of payment responses on the public graph. A coefficient-event construction has polynomial rational bit complexity. A backward behavioral equivalence then characterizes the smallest writable alphabet implementing the unique optimum, including randomized exact-optimum controllers under an explicit information-accounting convention. For a heterogeneous renewal family, we derive an ordered memory-value frontier and prove that greater outside-option dispersion at a fixed mean payment strictly increases the loss from insufficient memory. A shared-table comparator has a graph-sized convex formulation, and fixed positive-release queries admit exact supporting cuts even at coincident bounds. Independent tree allocation, sparse convex optimization, and public-graph promise discretization provide algorithmic comparisons. Exact scale tests include more than two thousand public vertices and a separate breakpoint-dense family. The broader switching and certified-deployment theory is preserved in supplementary volumes.
