\section{Antecedent Computational Evidence}\label{sec:study54}
The following study is retained from the complete predecessor revision. Its protocols, source hashes, raw records and unsuccessful runs are unchanged; its timings are not pooled with the new executions in Section~\ref{sec:study56}. Its original article tables are reproduced in the electronic companion.

\subsection{Protocol and limits}
The fixed study contains 30 cases and 142 applicable method runs. One-factor sweeps vary histories through 768, catalog size through 65, command budget through 12, absolute accuracy through $10^{-6}$, and opening-charge scale. Four cases have heterogeneous terminal rewards; six are the original difficult historical models. Caps, probabilities, service curvatures, and ceilings are heterogeneous. Repeated base cases in different sweeps are intentional paired comparisons, not independent statistical observations. Table~\ref{tab:scale54} reports every new price-path case; Table~\ref{tab:fine54} gives the paired historical reruns.

Each method executes in a fresh process with one thread, a four-second algorithm-time alarm and a 2-GiB address-space cap. Imports are timed separately; peak resident memory is measured rather than assumed equal. The mixed-integer reference uses the original support-indicator formulation with 32-segment tangent and secant service envelopes and a two-second solver limit for each call. Total construction and both solves remain subject to the common alarm. A native solver can delay delivery of a Python signal; a separate five-second algorithm-phase process limit records that outcome. This distinction, actual elapsed time, and every limit event are retained.

The baselines are the inherited uniform-resource grid, exact book enumeration, class-mean box subdivision, and the direct numerical mixed-integer model. The inherited grid and box implementations support common rewards only and are not run on the four heterogeneous-reward cases. The price method makes at most 12 price attempts per node and evaluates at most 255 nodes. Rational proof checking is separate, with a 60-second verification alarm. An interrupted old routine is not assigned its unfinished computations as a proof: only an independently feasible incumbent and a universal upper bound survive unless the routine has returned a checkable certificate. Numerical mixed-integer status is never interpreted as rational certification. The full limits and input hashes were committed before these runs.

\subsection{Scaling, accuracy, and unsuccessful cases}
Across the original study, price paths produce \PriceExact{} exact closures, \PriceTolerance{} positive-width tolerance completions, and \PriceLimited{} resource-limited outcomes. All \PriceChecked{} resulting price certificates pass independent verification. Table~\ref{tab:outcomes54} retains every method's outcome. The larger-catalog cases demonstrate performance beyond what the allotted exhaustive enumeration can complete; they do not establish polynomial worst-case exact search. The largest history case illustrates the remaining limit. Its enclosing interval is reported even when it misses the requested tolerance.

The historical comparison is no longer between newly timed code and a different publication environment. All five applicable methods are rerun under the same caps. Enumeration remains attractive on these small catalogs, and numerical mixed-integer solutions can be fast. The benefit of the new path oracle is a substantially smaller exact-rational proof workload than the uniform resource grid on these cases, not a universal ordering of solver times. Compressed price certificates in the full study range from \PriceMinBytes{} to \PriceMaxBytes{} bytes. Table~\ref{tab:scale54} also reports checking time and peak process memory; small proof files do not imply zero verification cost.

Ten separate zero-width runs expose the search's anytime behavior in Table~\ref{tab:anytime54}. They include all six historical models and the largest two catalog and budget configurations. Of these, \AnytimeExact{} close exactly within the stated limits. Every per-oracle and per-node checkpoint remains in the execution record. Unlike a uniform grid, a smaller requested tolerance does not necessarily require more resource states: a supporting price or a completed discrete partition can close an interval exactly. The construction's worst-case guarantee remains finite exponential search, as stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:branch54}.

After deriving the complexity theorem, we separately froze four SUBSET SUM challenge configurations before executing them. Their outcomes occupy the lower panel of Table~\ref{tab:outcomes54}. These are deliberately difficult structural cases, not representative operational inputs. The source integer weights are even and the target odd; integer subset-sum dynamic programming supplies their exact reference distance. The new method also encounters node and time limits on this family. These failures are informative: the price bound does not remove the discrete obstruction proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:hard54}. Grid and box comparisons use width $1/(8K)$, while price and enumeration request exact closure, as explicitly declared in the addendum; their success counts are not pooled as identical-accuracy outcomes.

\subsection{Screening and independent correctness checks}
Table~\ref{tab:screen54} reports all 24 screening cases, crossing eight nonuniform-cap models and charge multipliers $0.9,1,1.1$ around the target-box envelope. The catalogs include several possible low anchors and a mixture of retained and deleted commands; promises and budgets vary. Exact inclusion and exclusion optima identify useful, tied, and strictly irrelevant candidates. Forced price bounds remove \ForcedAnchors{} possible-anchor occurrences across these cases. Rates, missed strictly irrelevant commands, wholly ineligible removals, and every charge slack are recorded. The stronger test incurs incumbent-search and bound-checking costs; no generic preprocessing speedup or removal guarantee is inferred. Three additional sharpness configurations make the same command necessary below the exclusion threshold, tied at equality, and excluded above it.

The regression suite verifies 130 models, including 60 common-cap models and 30 heterogeneous-reward models. All 130 global price intervals close at the exhaustive optimum. Independent singleton/pair certificates pass on the 60 common-cap cases, 40 of which have more than one eligibility class. The suite checks 2,644 fixed books, 390 restricted-price oracles, 2,035 nested-layer reconstructions, and the heterogeneous component construction and optimal component equality on all 2,644 books. A deliberate positive root-duality-gap example requires branching; a one-node interruption retains a valid incomplete-width certificate. Nine altered certificates are rejected. The separately declared hardness validation checks all \HardnessSubsets{} optional subsets on 60 reductions and all \HardnessBooks{} feasible catalog books in the smaller reductions. Every distance identity and yes/no equivalence agrees. These finite checks verify implementation consistency; the universal statements rest on the proofs.
