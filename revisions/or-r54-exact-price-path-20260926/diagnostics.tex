\section{Current Execution Scope and Detailed Diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostics54}
The current R54 study is distinct from the inherited R52 and R53 experiments. Its original protocol preceded the regression and 142-method comparison. A later addendum records the SUBSET SUM reduction and the heterogeneous component-packing extension, both derived during this revision. The addendum preceded the reduction checks and four structural challenge runs. Packing checks use the already declared regression models and are identified as post-derivation development checks, not a new unseen sample. The three exact sharpness neighbors are also separate development examples. No previous result is overwritten by these additions.

The resource cap is enforced as a process address-space limit of 2 GiB. Resident memory is an outcome, measured by the operating system, and is not claimed to be identically allocated across methods. Each method is run in its own process. Scientific imports are timed before the algorithm alarm and reported separately. Certificates are serialized after the algorithm stage, then independently verified. Time-limited compiled numerical routines can defer a Python signal; the parent process imposes an additional algorithm-phase wall-clock cutoff. A numerical solve that returns after an alarm is not silently counted as within the requested budget. The raw records contain actual times, the last completed phase, and process-limit statuses.

For an interrupted enumeration, completed books may improve the feasible incumbent, but unvisited books require the universal upper bound. For an interrupted uniform or class-box routine that has not returned a complete witness, a fallback original feasible policy is retained. Those wide fallback intervals are not advertised as the internal performance of a partially executed optimizer. In contrast, the new method keeps a complete cover throughout and returns its latest valid price-tree bound after a controlled interruption. Every source-model digest, requested tolerance, method limit and final evidence scope is included in the records.

Table~\ref{tab:mip54} separates actual numerical solver time, solver time allowance, analytical envelope error, and the signed numerical bracket width. The machine-readable records also retain every probability, service assignment, installed support, primal and dual value, status, relative solver gap and maximum constraint residual. Small negative floating-point widths are reported as roundoff, not rounded into an exact-proof claim. The new checker uses rational arithmetic and explicit exceptions; the old grid and box checkers retain their documented requirement not to disable assertions.

Table~\ref{tab:threshold54} gives every target-command threshold slack in the screening benchmark. For each of the eight commands in each case, the raw record additionally gives exact inclusion/exclusion values, the two safe-test decisions, possible-anchor status and whether the command is wholly ineligible. An omitted command from one displayed winning book need not be irrelevant: the inclusion reference determines whether some equally good book uses it. Strict irrelevance means its forced optimum is strictly below the global reference; the generated diagnostics count each test's missed strictly irrelevant commands. The benchmark does not claim to recover a mixed-integer solver's internal presolve-removal rate. It compares a cheap uniform deletion rule with stronger original-instance price fixing and with exact relevance.

The sharpness paragraph in the retained anchor-envelope proof requires a feasible singleton and pair, $m\geq2$, and $B=\bar b$. Under those conditions, the target vector is fixed at all caps and the pair-minus-singleton gross difference equals the envelope. Without them, the envelope remains safe but need not be attained at the accepted promise. Table~\ref{tab:production54} contains three exact neighbors of such a configuration. All have the same nonuniform caps and reward/service primitives; changing only the opening charge changes necessity, a tie, and strict exclusion. This resolves the distinction between safety of the equality test and exclusion from every optimum.

For context, the retained R53 controlled screen used width $1/10$ and transferred the interval $[15/64,91/320]$. Its width is $1/20$, or $16/75\approx21.33\%$ of the reference $15/64$. Its exact optimum was supplied by enumeration, not by that positive-width interval. Those historical cases and their wholly ineligible endpoint removal remain intact; they are not substituted for the present near-threshold evaluation.

\section{Independent Production Configuration Example}
The retained production theorem applies to a compatibility graph whose selected modules share divisible load in parallel. For the current numerical example, the capacity potentials are $(0,2,5,9)$ and all increasing vertex pairs are compatible. For edges $(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,2),(1,3),(2,3)$, the respective triples of linear return, quadratic curvature, and opening payment are
\[
 (5,1,1/2),\ (4,1/2,1),\ (3,1/2,0),\ (7,2,3/2),\ (9/2,1,1/4),\ (6,3/2,3/4).
\]
Each edge's capacity is the difference of its endpoint potentials. The accepted orders are $3,6,8$, crossed with module budgets $1,2,3$. Every feasible configuration is enumerated; clipped stationary allocations and a common rational supporting price prove its exact operating optimum. Table~\ref{tab:production54} displays all nine optimum configurations. Full allocations, all alternative paths and exact values are retained. These module-specific returns and capacity coordinates were specified independently, with no mapping from renewal histories or terminal lotteries. The example demonstrates the capacity-conservative abstraction numerically; it does not assert an observed production installation or estimated economic parameters.

\section{Verification of Original-Instance Binding}
Price certificates use a canonical rational model representation and SHA-256 of its sorted JSON encoding. A verifier can supply the expected digest from the requested model record. It then checks mathematical feasibility and all price-tree bounds, not merely the digest. Different encodings must first be converted to the documented canonical representation; a certificate's self-declared hash does not authenticate an external model. The budget is retained as supplied, including budgets exceeding the catalog size, while the recurrence clips its effective command count. A separate regression checks that normalization does not silently change this instance binding.

The singleton/pair certificate contains every feasible singleton and pair permitted by the budget and a fixed-book supporting price for each. Its independent checker evaluates the original scalar responses at eligible knots, individual caps and clipped stationary tail points. Their supporting bounds, together with one original feasible policy attaining their maximum, establish the exact value under the common-cap theorem. No grid, search-tree completeness flag or numerical status is used as a substitute for this coverage.
