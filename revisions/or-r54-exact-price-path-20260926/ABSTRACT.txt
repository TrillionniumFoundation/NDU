A renewal provider chooses a paid, limited catalog of terminal commands and allocates an accepted obligation across heterogeneous histories. We derive exact selected-boundary resource and price representations that preserve individual expected caps, realization ceilings, and the original promise. History-level component packing extends the representations to heterogeneous increasing concave terminal rewards. The rational decision problem is NP-complete even with unit linear reward, zero service costs, uniform probabilities, and a nonbinding command budget. In contrast, common expected caps admit an exact two-command reduction under a common reward. For rational quadratic primitives, a price oracle is quadratic in catalog size and linear in history count and command budget. It supports grid-free finite exact branching and independently verified intervals, including interrupted runs. A polynomial additive resource scheme remains available, with cubic catalog and quadratic budget dependence after substituting inverse normalized accuracy. Forced-command price bounds also permit safe exclusion of possible anchors. Matched-limit synthetic experiments vary history count, catalog size, budget, accuracy, and charges; exact reduction challenges expose unsuccessful cases as well as favorable performance. The contribution is the structural reduction, complexity boundary, and original-instance certification, not an empirical claim of universal solver superiority.
