\section{Conclusion}
A hard execution-memory budget need not be replaced by an enlarged union in order to obtain a constructive guarantee. The target-crossing decomposition optimizes every alphabet budget exactly once branch targets are given. It yields global polynomial catalog design at saturation and, through a feasible weighted target net, arbitrary additive accuracy at the original budget when the number of branches is fixed. At larger branch counts, local distortion certificates and charge-aware support compression provide useful feasible policies and explicitly measured gaps without claiming that conditional optimality solves target selection.

The results retain the renewal model's commitments: the interface carries a fixed finite alphabet, intermediate service precedes the terminal draw, and participation and realization restrictions remain separate. An execution symbol represents one enabled terminal command, while its writable bit requirement is the logarithm of the alphabet size. Opening charges model certification or maintenance of those commands; their level must be supplied or estimated for a particular institution. Zero charges describe an available interface whose extra enabled commands are treated as costless, not a factual assertion that operational memory is free. The same mathematics applies to an ordered service gateway with branch demand caps, preset command levels, an advance-service cost, and per-realization safety limits, but this is a second interpretation of the abstract model, not independent empirical validation.

The evidence supports exact conditional computation and the stated approximation and repair guarantees. The charged target-portfolio counterexample shows that a good feasible policy can still require a different target profile. A tight universal union expansion factor and scalable global approximation with an unrestricted number of branches are not established here. They are not prerequisites for the original-budget saturation theorem, the fixed-branch additive scheme, or the local certificates proved in this article.
