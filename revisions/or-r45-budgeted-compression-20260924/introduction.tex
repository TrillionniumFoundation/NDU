\section{Introduction}
An execution interface may support only a few certified service levels even when a planner can distinguish many customer histories. Designing such an interface is not just a forecasting problem. The planner must select the levels, allocate an accepted aggregate obligation across branches, and respect both participation limits and the largest permitted realization. Enabling another level may also require certification or maintenance expenditure. We study these decisions jointly for a renewal provider with concave terminal reward and heterogeneous convex intermediate-service costs.

The central question is how to optimize within the stated symbol budget. An inexpensive pricing problem does not by itself answer this question: its supporting policies can require different alphabets, and the price bound can have a positive gap. Taking their union implements a useful resource-augmentation policy, but may exceed the permitted memory and incur unnecessary opening charges. We instead use the ordered interface twice. First, once the branch targets are specified, an eligibility-aware path dynamic program optimizes the alphabet at every symbol budget, including all opening charges. Second, a feasible net of branch targets converts this conditional optimizer into an additive approximation scheme for the original joint problem when the number of branches is fixed. Neither step randomizes the installed alphabet or relaxes the accepted promise.

The first result is an exact budgeted compression theorem. Consecutive installed levels partition the branches by their targets. An edge contributes either an eligible adjacent lottery or a fixed intermediate-service tail; the distinction depends on each branch's realization ceiling. One dynamic program computes the entire conditional memory--value frontier. At a saturated aggregate obligation the targets are forced, so the same algorithm is a polynomial global catalog optimizer with arbitrary nonnegative level charges and heterogeneous realization ceilings. Away from saturation, conditional optimality is not joint optimality. The second result addresses this remaining target choice directly: a weighted target net with exact feasibility repair gives any prescribed additive accuracy without increasing the symbol budget. Its accuracy dependence has an exponent determined by the branch count, which we state explicitly rather than describing it as a polynomial algorithm for an unrestricted number of branches.

A complementary set of certificates makes the computation informative before such a net becomes affordable. Capped ideal targets give a Jensen upper bound. Optimizing their alphabet yields a same-budget lower policy and an instance-dependent distortion-and-charge certificate. Local interpolation errors replace a uniform mesh constant when the relevant targets and cells are known. In the absence of charges, uniform comparison books give first-order accuracy in the inverse symbol count, and second-order accuracy when the realization headroom accommodates adjacent lotteries. These bounds compare policies at the original budget; they do not require the additional symbols used by union recovery.

The price oracle remains valuable. We compress its feasible target mixtures at every desired budget, optimize the charge--distortion tradeoff rather than pay the whole union charge, and compare the result with existing feasible policies. An inexact-oracle theorem separates support suboptimality, price error, compression loss, and target-repair error. It requires certified price bounds and feasible endpoint policies, not an unjustified monotonicity rule for approximate optimizers. The exact continuous quadratic reduction, the saturated Monge results, and the institutional comparisons are retained as complementary parts of the theory.

Our computational study distinguishes correctness, optimization quality, and application evidence. Exact conditional comparisons use an independent enumeration of supports and books. A second formulation keeps the original probability, intermediate-service, activation, and realization constraints in a mixed-integer model, without using the canonical response or either dynamic program. Tangent and secant envelopes provide numerical brackets that are compared with exact catalog optima. Across six seeds and three cap families, the core target portfolio attains the original-budget optimum in thirty-five of thirty-six instances; an explicitly retained charged counterexample is solved by the greedy fallback. Larger heterogeneous instances test conditional scaling, not unproved joint optimality. Adaptive-catalog comparisons use exact continuous references only in regimes where those references are available. All settings are synthetic; no field calibration or operational acceptance of additional memory is asserted.

\subsection{Relation to established optimization methods}\label{sec:r37-position}
Finite policy graphs separate a rich history from a finite implementation interface \citep{Zhang2012}, while quantized feedback control studies the consequences of finite command sets \citep{Fu2024}. Optimal scalar quantization and dual quantization provide established methods for choosing levels and unbiased adjacent randomizations \citep{Wu1991,PagesWilbertz2012,JourdainPages2021}. Our distortion is not nearest-neighbor error: a skipped eligible level changes an adjacent lottery, while an ineligible upper endpoint creates a pre-draw intermediate-service obligation. Its economic cost and feasible support therefore depend on the branch target and realization limit.

Interval dynamic programming is classical, including the scalar segmentation framework of \citet{NielsenNock2014}. We do not claim the generic path recurrence, a target net, or convexification as new mechanisms. The structural content is the exact target-crossing decomposition under contractual timing and heterogeneous eligibility, and its use as a polynomial conditional oracle inside a same-budget global approximation scheme. Lagrangian bounds and constrained-path recovery are also established methods \citep{HandlerZang1980,Lemarechal2001}. Here their role is explicitly separate from feasibility: the union lemma makes mixtures implementable, whereas the new compression and target-net results retain the original resource cap. The quadratic stationary-face method supplies an exact, exponential reference for unrestricted continuous design rather than a competing polynomial-time claim \citep{DelPiaEtAl2017}.

The article develops the model, the conditional frontier, and the same-budget guarantees before reporting comparative computation. The appendices and electronic companion retain the continuous and saturated theory, detailed proofs, and reproducibility specifications. Earlier derivations and executed studies remain available in immutable source directories and in the complete predecessor reader package; the preservation map distinguishes archival evidence from newly executed experiments.
