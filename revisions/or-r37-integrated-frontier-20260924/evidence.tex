\section{Verification and Computational Decision Support}\label{sec:r37-evidence}
The computational study has three distinct targets: correctness of the implemented interfaces, practical costs of alternative implementations, and evaluation of a finite reduced model by an external solver. It does not calibrate demand or estimate customers' willingness to accept realization variation. The deployment mechanism in Section~\ref{sec:r37-institution} and its prices are stipulated design inputs.

\paragraph{Exact checks.}
The new tests enumerate monotone cutoffs in both completion directions, including wholly padded selected rows, finite ties, signed column offsets, and every nonempty row and column submatrix through dimension four. They compare direct minima with both the retained implementation and the independently authored offline search in \citet{EppsteinPADS}. Separate heterogeneous rational instances compare all budgets against the quadratic and divide-and-conquer engines and replay every returned controller directly. Bounded-overrun tests use an independent all-pairs lottery calculation and include nonanchored codebook grid checks. The proofs, rather than these finite tests, establish the continuous and all-size statements.

\paragraph{Scale and memory.}
The same-input study extends the inherited family to 16,384 branches. For branch index $j=1,\ldots,k$, it uses $b_j=j/(k+1)$, $\pi_j=2j/[k(k+1)]$, $\gamma_j=(j\bmod5)/3$, and $r=2,q=1$, computing budgets one through eight. Each engine and institution runs in a fresh process with an explicit 120-second process limit. A limit observation is reported as such, never as a measured frontier time. Table~\ref{tab:r37-scale} reports unprofiled time, economic requests, and peak resident memory. The independent PADS comparison substitutes search alone; economic moments and reconstruction remain shared and are not mislabeled independent algorithms.

Python reduced-fraction arithmetic makes exact equality and coefficient growth auditable, but it is not a performance model for an optimized native implementation. Independent heap-traced runs measure Python-tracked allocations; resident peaks also include the interpreter and imported code. Separately profiled runs decompose exclusive time into rational arithmetic, economic-oracle work, matrix-search/key bookkeeping, and the combined frontier-scan/reconstruction routine. The last category is deliberately not presented as a separately measured reconstruction cost. The companion gives the measurements and definitions.

\paragraph{External finite-model comparison.}
We also assemble unit-flow networks directly from branch sums, without the moment oracle, and solve them with compiled HiGHS dual simplex through SciPy \citep{HuangfuHall2018,Virtanen2020}. The expected-participation network uses the proved continuous terminal minimizations, not a discretization of the highest level. HiGHS operates in floating point. We extract its integral path, reprice it in exact fractions, verify the controller, and certify its reduced-network optimum by exact shortest-path potentials. Table~\ref{tab:r37-solver} separates graph construction, solver time, and exact certification plus replay. This is an external solver comparison for the finite reduction, not a claim that the solver independently proves cap anchoring or that native SMAWK has been benchmarked.

The earlier experiments, including unfavorable small-instance search constants and shared-cache results, remain unchanged in the preservation archive. The new evidence addresses the remaining implementation questions rather than accumulate synthetic examples as a substitute for operational data. In particular, finite-precision wall time, rational-operation complexity, and contract value are reported as different quantities.
