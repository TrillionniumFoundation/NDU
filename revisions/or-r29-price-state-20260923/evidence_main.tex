\section{Exact Computational Evidence}\label{sec:evidence}
The new experiment operates on one coupled, recombining stochastic graph per instance. A root has two or three possible successors, and every subsequent public regime can transition to every next-period regime. Rewards, curvatures, payment coefficients, and continuation caps vary with date and regime. The exact root promise couples decisions across all reachable successors. The experiment does not increase dimension by replicating independent two-period blocks.

The main response suite contains 35 instances, including 64 decision dates, fixed shared-table corridors, root promises on both boundary tails, a negative equality price, tied barriers, fixed actions, and a cap binding at its minimum feasible payment. The independent verifier checks entire piecewise-affine response identities, all reached state flows and local KKT conditions, conditional participation, root payment, and equality of the implemented reward with its dual upper bound. It performs 147,157 exact assertion groups. Two deliberately infeasible inputs are rejected. The full certificate files, not only summary statistics, are retained.

Table~\ref{tab:r29-scaling} reports the main scaling observations. The 64-date, three-regime instance has 190 public vertices, 332 distinct response knots, and 580 reached price pairs. Its ternary unfolding would contain $(3^{64}-1)/2$ decision histories. No such tree is constructed by the production solver. The response construction and independent audit took approximately \PriceSolveSeconds{} and \PriceAuditSeconds{} seconds in the recorded run; these are single-run Python rational-arithmetic observations, not a speedup estimate or a claim about a tuned general-purpose solver. Coefficient bit lengths and serialized certificate sizes are reported alongside counts. The numerical evidence supplements the proved graph-size bound; it is not used to infer worst-case complexity.

Fourteen small instances are separately unfolded. Their conditional-incidence matrices satisfy exact rational primal feasibility, stationarity, and complementarity. A feasibility linear program followed by a floating-point convex quadratic solve produces a largest absolute value discrepancy of approximately $\TreeDiscrepancy$. The numerical solver is only a cross-check: its termination flag is not the certificate. Eleven optimized time-only table comparators are solved and verified by exact rational KKT conditions on the independently reconstructed graph-sized quadratic programs. Their nonnegative adaptation premiums compare two optimized classes on identical primitives.

A further 54 perturbed table/release queries satisfy the exact supporting-cut inequalities, and a two-service instance checks the scalar-commitment extension. Eight memory families reach 32 distinct renewal regimes; all 277 set partitions for sizes two through six are enumerated independently to check the ordered-cell frontier. Table~\ref{tab:r29-memory} gives the eight-regime frontier. No implementation costs are fitted to make a chosen memory architecture appear optimal.

The inherited evidence is retained as a separate, fully typeset computational appendix, with its original source, raw records, and interpretation. In particular, the 83/84 cache-refresh result and the absence of a measured cache speed advantage remain adverse findings. The prior learned routes also retain their unfavorable matched-quality results. Those observations concern different algorithms and designs; they are neither erased nor relabeled as measurements of finite-price computation. EC Section~\ref{ec:r29-checks} specifies the new design and verification boundaries.
