# Closest results and residual theorem — R29

The statements below identify what is and is not claimed. They are a research comparison, not a certification of priority.

| Established result class | What is inherited | Residual result in this revision | Essential boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nested Benders, policy aggregation, SDDP; Benders (1962), Rockafellar–Wets (1991), Pereira–Pinto (1991) | A shared design variable, nonanticipativity, dual cuts and a master are standard. | Theorem 4.1 constructs the *complete payment response* directly from a compact graph; each cap adds at most one knot. | Positive scalar additive payment; separable rewards; no intertemporal switching. |
| Convex multistage convergence; Girardeau–Leclere–Philpott (2015) | Convergence of convex decomposition is not new. | An exact finite breakpoint union and explicit graph-input arithmetic/storage bound, not asymptotic convergence of sampled cuts. | Finite acyclic public graph and the specified response closure. |
| Policy graph decomposition; Dowson (2020) | Recombining graph models and SDDP on policy graphs are standard. | A derived finite price augmentation with at most N+1 labels, and a matching linear-order necessary-memory family. | Date/regime/public memory already sufficient for exogenous primitives; scalar ordered prices. |
| Modern SDDP variants and complexity; Fullner–Rebennack (2025), Lan (2023) | Modern methods already have convergence/complexity results under their assumptions. | Direct exact cap-reflection construction, with no discovered full-tree dual or unspecified cut count. | Arithmetic bound, not bit complexity or an exact generic SDDP bound. |
| Nested separable resource allocation; Vidal–Jaillet–Maculan (2016), Schoot Uiterkamp–Hurink–Gerards (2021), Wu–Nip–He (2021) | Scalar resource prices, water filling, nested allocation, breakpoint methods are established. | Global knot-union bound on a compact recombining stochastic graph, its realized finite-memory contract, and the continuation-cap memory-value theorem. | Not a claim to improve the cited chain/allocation complexities on their own input models. |
| Parametric convex quadratic programming | Active-set KKT and exact table-QP solving are standard. | Proposition 6.1 identifies a public-graph-sized formulation of the optimized zero-release shared contract, including edge switching. | Zero release; global table fixed consistently across histories. |
| Supporting cuts for shared-design optimization | Sensitivity prices and Benders masters are standard. | Proposition 6.2 supplies exact fixed-table positive-release values from the finite-price engine, without full-tree solving. | No finite outer cut-count theorem; fixed-table oracle is distinct from globally optimizing the master. |
| Segmentation and finite-state implementation | Dynamic programming over ordered groups is standard. | Theorem 5.1 derives the group loss and proves contiguous optimal grouping from binding continuation primitives, as well as necessary terminal memory. | Stated three-date family; mutable label bits exclude table storage/precision. |
| Cache governance, parametric release, Bernstein certificates | Retained R24–R28 results and their standard foundations. | No new cache speed or refresh-frequency claim. Their role is supporting general-model theory and implementation audit. | Original assumptions unchanged; negative evidence retained. |

The new theorem's proof may use standard convex duality, but its finite response closure and operational memory characterization are the conclusions to assess. We expressly separate a new specialization theorem from a claim of a new general duality principle.
