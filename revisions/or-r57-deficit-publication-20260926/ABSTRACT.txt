A renewal provider selects a paid, limited catalog of terminal commands and allocates an accepted obligation across histories with individual expected caps and realization ceilings. We derive exact selected-boundary resource and price representations, including heterogeneous increasing concave terminal rewards. Under a common reward, an optimal book needs at most twice the number of distinct expected caps, and this bound is tight. A constructive extension groups histories jointly by cap and catalog reward type. Complementing edge resources merges all feasible anchors into one deficit dynamic program. Centered concave kernels and exact original-promise repair give an additive approximation with quadratic rather than cubic catalog dependence. With zero service cost, rational component capacities yield exact lattice optimization at arbitrary command budgets. Its pseudo-polynomial dependence on scaled granularity explains the weak SUBSET SUM hardness of a normalized subclass without asserting polynomial binary-input complexity. Exact price paths additionally support finite branching and independently checked original-instance intervals. Synthetic experiments separate positive-tolerance and exact-target runs, expose search mechanics, retain resource limits, and measure screening plus downstream verification. A service-credit architecture and a payoff-calibration interface distinguish operational implementation from empirical calibration.
