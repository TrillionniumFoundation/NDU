## 12.1. Complexity frontier: a positive variable-history result

The report correctly identifies that the old target net does not explain the role of history count. New Theorem 7.3 supplies an alternative to a hardness claim. For rational quadratic primitives, bounded reward and shortfall-curvature scales, a fixed number of eligible catalog prefixes, and any fixed positive additive accuracy, joint finite-catalog design is polynomial in the number of histories, catalog size, command budget, and rational input length. The construction bins caps and curvatures, keeps eligibility exact, and isolates a minimum-cap anchor guard. Lemma 7.2 bounds the reduction error and the number of reduced groups independently of raw history count. The proof gives an explicit conservative dyadic node bound. This is not a fully polynomial approximation scheme: dependence on accuracy and eligibility complexity can be very large. No NP-hardness classification is asserted. The inherited fixed-budget enumeration, saturation, exact-type, and continuous-design regimes remain in the manuscript; the provenance table separates these from the four R47 statements.

## 12.2. Structural global method and feasible disaggregation

R46 supplied the two-sided price-path decomposition and adaptive target-box cover, replacing the full target grid as the main joint algorithm. R47 extends this mechanism beyond exactly repeated histories. Theorem 7.1 uses weighted mean caps and the minimum cost curvature within a common eligibility group to obtain an optimistic joint problem. The mean-cap construction preserves total capacity, including saturated promises. It is not used as a substitute for original individual caps. A clip-and-fill lift preserves each group mean, restores each individual cap, and executes the same selected commands at the same opening charge. Its exact weighted transfer identity yields both a posterior error bound and a uniform bound. The theorem sandwiches the original optimum between a feasible original policy and the reduced upper bound. Proposition 7.4 proves monotonicity of the optimistic optima under refinement; extrema of all valid bounds give a nested interval even when individual solves stop early. A minimum-cap counterexample demonstrates why unrestricted averaging is invalid.

## 12.3. Nontrivial accuracy and documented limits

The new study executes 24 specified joint-design instances at absolute tolerance 0.001, with 4 through 256 distinct histories, 9 or 17 catalog levels, budgets two or three, one or four eligible prefixes, and charged as well as uncharged instances. All 24 coarsened policies obtain a checked original-space interval of width at most 0.001; the largest width is approximately 0.000783098. Each 256-history input has 256 distinct exact response types, not replicated branches. These are clustered synthetic perturbations and are not evidence that all populations admit five useful groups. The conservative accuracy bins in Theorem 7.3 can be much finer than the successful practical bins; practical completion is accepted only from the checked original-space interval. The inherited R46 grid study, including incomplete nets and their withheld accuracy guarantees, remains intact in the companion. The new claim is not that exhaustive nets became practical.

## 12.4. Failures, heuristic distinctions, and refinement

The R47 headline concerns joint upper and lower certificates, not target-portfolio exact-hit counts. A separate, disclosed supplementary protocol contains four deliberately dispersed three-history examples. In all four, the reduced problem is solved but the original requested tolerance is not met: initial gaps are 0.0421875, 0.044375, 0.11, and 0.67. Splitting the remaining mixed group produces exact original solutions in these four examples. The manuscript retains both stages, rather than discarding the unresolved coarse relaxations. These examples establish neither a universal one-split bound nor general heuristic exactness. The older portfolio results and the charged failure remain historical evidence, with their corrected sample-only labels. All 24 primary/scaling outcomes, all eight stress stages, and the corresponding exact certificates are retained.

## 12.5. Independent comparison and reproducibility

The 24 new cases compare coarsening, the uncoarsened R46 box method, and the inherited priced-prefix method under the same original objective and requested tolerance, with common nominal node and soft time limits. They complete 24, 19, and 21 cases respectively. Median measured local times are approximately 0.0375, 0.3183, and 0.1691 seconds. Soft time checks occur between oracle blocks and are documented rather than presented as hard deadlines. Every primary case also has an all-book joint reference; this reference shares the exact allocator and is labeled accordingly. A separate uneliminated mixed-integer formulation is run with both quadratic envelopes on all 12 primary cases. All 24 solves return optimal numerical status; per-case time, nodes, numerical primal-dual gap, envelope error, bracket width, and residual information are saved. Floating-point MIP bounds are not rational certificates. Original local timings remain separate from subsequent publication replay timings. R46's stronger priced-prefix outcomes and its four interrupted box cases remain in the companion.

## 12.6. Stronger certificates in the original history space

The reduced upper bound optimizes the shared command book and endogenous targets; it is not a Jensen bound at a prespecified target vector. The new posterior loss separates cap-restoration movement from cost dispersion, and charges cancel because the lift preserves the book. An independently implemented checker recomputes the representative data, guard, eligibility, error bounds, exact group masses, lower value, all original lottery constraints, and the inner Bellman/coverage certificate. It neither imports the coarsening optimizer nor trusts its completion flag. The test suite compares 72 exact original/reduced joint optima, 602 book-specific lifts, 441 cap-dispersion inequalities, 216 accuracy partitions, and 56 refinement comparisons. Eighteen semantic certificate corruptions are rejected. The older inexact-support theorem retains its explicit strong-oracle assumptions; it is not relabeled as an unconditional heuristic guarantee.
